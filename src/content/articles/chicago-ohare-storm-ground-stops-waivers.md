Severe thunderstorms over the Chicago area forced another round of FAA ground stops and a ground delay program at O'Hare International Airport (ORD) on Tuesday, August 11th, the third straight day of storm-driven stoppages at the United and American Airlines hub. Midway (MDW) also took a ground stop. As of Wednesday morning, August 12th, the [FAA’s O'Hare status page](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=ORD&p=1) showed only light gate-hold and airborne delays of 15 minutes or less, but residual schedule mess and airline rebooking windows still matter if your trip touches Chicago.

This is the same check-your-booking habit as other summer disruption weeks, including [Northeast storm and controller-staffing delays](/news/faa-northeast-ground-stops-atc-staffing/) and [wildfire-smoke slowdowns](/news/canadian-wildfire-smoke-us-canada-flights/).

## What happened

Thunderstorms hit the Chicago metro hard enough for the FAA to stop and meter traffic into O'Hare across August 9th, 10th, and 11th. On Tuesday morning, a ground stop held flights bound for O'Hare during the worst cells. Separate departure restrictions and an evening ground delay program stretched outbound and inbound traffic (reported average delays around 144 minutes, or about 2.4 hours). Midway faced its own thunderstorm ground stop the same day.

Flight-tracking tallies put O'Hare cancellations in the high hundreds across those three days, with ORD topping US airport cancellation lists on Tuesday and more than 1,500 delays reported there that day. United kept cancellations relatively low at the hub but carried the biggest delay pile (hundreds of delayed United flights out of Chicago). Regional partners such as SkyWest, Republic, and Envoy also took large cancellation hits.

Wednesday morning looked quieter on the live FAA airport board, and early flight-tracking snapshots showed only a small single-digit cancellation count at ORD. Aircraft, crews, and bags can still be out of position after three storm days.

## What this means for travelers

If you flew, connected, or were supposed to fly through O'Hare or Midway on August 9th through 11th, you could still face knock-on disruption even when the status board looks calm. Missed connections and late aircraft can linger after the sky clears.

Several carriers opened temporary fee-free change tools during the storm cycle. Check your airline’s published waiver window in manage booking rather than assuming every mid-August Chicago booking qualifies. Those windows are short, carrier-specific, and easy to miss if you only look at a default change-fee quote.

I have a Chicago connection later this week, so I am watching the same status boards and manage-booking screens as everyone else. A quieter Wednesday morning helps, but I am not treating a clear radar as a guarantee for that connection.

## Airline waivers still in play

[United’s travel alerts page](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/travel/trip-planning/travel-alerts.html) has carried a “Chicago Thunderstorms” weather exception for O'Hare. As published there during this storm window, United said it would waive change fees and fare differences for originally scheduled August 11th Chicago travel if you reschedule onto a United flight departing between August 9th, 2026 and August 14th, 2026, in the same cabin and between the same cities as originally booked. United’s Travel-Ready tools also showed the Chicago Thunderstorms waiver as active earlier in the week (waiver code 7JCU6 on that board). Only tickets that show an eligible option in manage booking qualify. Recheck that screen before you assume you still do.

American Airlines posted Chicago / O'Hare weather change-fee flexibility during the storm days (including a same-day August 9th ORD alert and later August 11th ORD rebooking messaging). As of Wednesday morning, August 12th, American’s public [travel alerts page](https://www.aa.com/web/i18n/travel-info/travel-alerts.html) no longer listed a Chicago weather alert. Open your trip in the American app or site and look for a fee-free change option there, or call if the online tool will not show one.

Other airlines that touch Chicago may have opened their own short windows. Rules differ by ticket purchase date, original travel date, allowed new travel dates, cabin, and city pair. Basic Economy can still be tighter even when a fee is waived.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled (airline app, alerts, or the airline site).
2. Check the [FAA National Airspace System status](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) and the [ORD airport status page](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=ORD&p=1) for ground stops or delay programs that may not show in the app right away.
3. Look for a travel waiver in manage booking before you pay a change fee. If you qualify and your plans are flexible, move while seats remain rather than waiting for the last rebook inventory.
4. If you hold a short connection through O'Hare, assume extra risk after a multi-day storm bank. Prefer a same-airline protected connection the carrier can reaccommodate.
5. If you are already at the airport, stay on the airline’s rebooking path and keep receipts for meals or hotels if you get stuck overnight.

Weather and ATC restrictions in the US generally do not trigger a standalone airline cash payment for a simple delay. Under [DOT refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), a cancellation or significant delay or schedule change is a different conversation if you choose not to travel. If you paid with a card that includes flight delay insurance, that coverage can still matter for hotel and meal costs after a long enough delay. For the practical checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

O'Hare took three straight days of thunderstorm ground stops and a ground delay program through August 11th. Wednesday morning ops looked much quieter, but residual disruption is real, and United’s published Chicago Thunderstorms window still allows eligible travelers to move to a United flight departing through August 14th for originally scheduled August 11th Chicago tickets that show the option in manage booking. Check your flight, check FAA status, and use a waiver there before you pay change fees.
