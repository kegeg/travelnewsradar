Catania-Fontanarossa Airport (CTA) is still getting hit by Mount Etna ash in the middle of Sicily’s peak summer rush. On the morning of **August 11th, 2026**, airport operator **SAC** extended the closure of ash-affected airspace sector **C1** and **suspended arrivals until 19:00 local time**, while **departures** were still allowed from **runway 08**. That follows a full overnight stop of arrivals and departures through **06:00** the same morning.

Italy travel already has another August wrinkle with [air and sea border checks on Spain-Italy hops](/news/italy-spain-schengen-border-checks/). This one is operational: your Catania flight can move, cancel, or divert with little notice while volcanic ash is still affecting the airspace.

## What changed at Catania

SAC runs Catania’s [Vincenzo Bellini / Fontanarossa airport](https://www.aeroporto.catania.it/). Through this Etna episode it has been rolling timed suspensions rather than a single multi-day shutdown.

On **August 10th**, arrivals were first halted into the afternoon, then stretched later, and by evening SAC had **paused both arrivals and departures until 06:00 on August 11th** after ash stayed in the airspace and also settled on the runway and across parts of the city. Local reports of that overnight notice also mention a second closed sector (**B3**) alongside the southwest ash cloud in **C1**.

By **August 11th morning**, the picture eased only partly: arrivals remained blocked into the evening (until **19:00** local), with departures still possible from runway 08. SAC’s repeated traveler line is simple: **do not go to the airport** until you have checked your flight status with the airline. Those windows can move again if ash or wind shifts.

Airlines have been diverting some Catania traffic to other Sicilian airports during this window, especially **Palermo (PMO)**, with **Comiso (CIY)** and **Trapani** also used on earlier days. A diversion can leave you hours from eastern Sicily by road even when you are “in Sicily.”

## Why Etna ash stops jets

Volcanic ash is bad for jet engines and for visibility. Controllers close the sectors under the cloud, and airports stop landings (and sometimes takeoffs) until the air and runway are safe again.

Italy’s National Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology (INGV) Etna Observatory raised the [Volcano Observatory Notice for Aviation (VONA) color code to red](https://www.ct.ingv.it/Dati/informative/vona/VONA_Etna_202608091419Z_2026008406E05.pdf) on **August 9th, 2026**, citing strong explosive activity at the summit craters, lava flows in Valle del Leone and Valle del Bove, and ash moving south / southeast. The Toulouse Volcanic Ash Advisory Center still had Etna at [aviation colour code red](https://vaac.meteo.fr/advisory/2026/211060_20260811100003/211060_20260811100003_vaa.txt) on **August 11th**, with observed ash from the surface up to about flight level 160 (roughly **4.9 km / 16,000 ft**) drifting southwest, with the highest concentration near the volcano.

Local INGV updates through this episode have focused on intense explosive activity at the **Voragine** crater and ongoing lava activity on Etna’s eastern flank. The aviation colour code can stay red even while SAC toggles airport windows hour by hour.

## What this means for travelers

If you are booked into or out of **CTA** around **August 11th–15th**, plan around live volcanic ash conditions and a schedule that can still move. **Ferragosto** falls on **August 15th**, so hotel demand, rental cars, and remaining seats may be tighter if you need a same-day pivot.

If you are already in eastern Sicily and trying to leave, watch for a departure that is still operating, a rebook onto a later CTA flight, or an airline diversion/re-ticket via Palermo or another Sicilian airport. Ground transfers between those airports are long. A free same-evening bus only helps if your airline or the region confirms it for your flight.

If you are still inbound to Catania, your aircraft may hold, divert, or cancel before you ever get near the island. Check status before you leave your connecting city, not only before the last taxi to Fontanarossa.

If your Sicily trip can use **Palermo** as plan B for the whole itinerary, that may be steadier than waiting on CTA during an active ash plume. Comiso is closer to some southeast Sicily bases but can have fewer onward public-transport options when flights dump there.

## What to do now

1. Open your booking and turn on airline alerts. Rebooking usually starts with **your airline**, before you consider another carrier.
2. Recheck the same day against SAC updates on the [Catania airport site](https://www.aeroporto.catania.it/) and your airline’s app. Morning windows have been changing into the evening.
3. If you are diverted, get the airline’s onward plan in writing (hotel, transfer, new flight) before you pay for a private transfer across Sicily.
4. If you get stuck overnight, keep receipts. Flight delay insurance on the card that paid for the ticket can cover hotel and meals after a long enough delay on many Canadian and US travel cards. Thresholds vary, so read your benefits guide once. For the broader disruption checklist, see [Flytrippers on canceled and delayed flights](https://flytrippers.com/most-important-things-related-to-canceled-and-delayed-flights/).

## Bottom line

Catania is still operating under rolling Etna ash limits days before Ferragosto. As of the morning of August 11th, SAC had arrivals suspended until 19:00 local while departures could still use runway 08, after a full overnight stop. Check your airline before you go to the airport, and plan for diversions or a Palermo backup if eastern Sicily is non-negotiable.
