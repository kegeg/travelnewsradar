Talks between Icelandair and its aircraft mechanics ended Friday without a deal, so an indefinite walkout is still set for **00:01 on Wednesday, September 23rd, 2026**, in Iceland (8:01 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, September 22nd). Flights are still operating. If the stoppage starts, Icelandair's current plan is that its scheduled flights from Iceland, international and domestic, would stop.

Another Europe labor disruption after [Charleroi's night controller walkout](/news/charleroi-airport-skeyes-night-atc-strike/). Icelandair's hub is Keflavik (KEF), so US and Canadian flights **operated by Icelandair** to, from, or through Iceland carry this risk.

## What changed Friday

[Icelandair Group's September 14th notice](https://storage.mfn.se/3d0d1f83-8282-4b6e-be31-14371b4bcd63/icelandair-aircraft-mechanics-announce-strike-action.pdf) said the Icelandic Aircraft Mechanics Association (FVFÍ) had called an indefinite strike of Icelandair mechanics from 00:01 on September 23rd. Unless the two sides reach a new contract before then, the airline said the action is expected to disrupt operations and may affect the flight schedule.

Bargaining teams met Friday at Iceland's state mediator, Ástráður Haraldsson. It was the first formal session since talks broke down on June 22nd. They left without an agreement. A formal mediation proposal, if one comes, would go to a vote. That step alone does not postpone the walkout unless the parties agree to pause it while members vote.

The remaining gap is pay. Union chair Óskar Einarsson has said Icelandair's offer is 3.5%, in line with the general Icelandic labor market, and that mechanics want a catch-up after a longer-term lag versus a comparison group. Icelandair CEO Bogi Nils Bogason has said mechanics already received raises in line with that market, and that the airline has gone as far as it can. The company has proposed independent arbitration on the pay item.

## Who would be affected

This dispute covers **Icelandair's own aircraft mechanics**.

The union said **88.3%** of those who voted approved the walkout, on **92%** turnout. Contracts have been open since New Year, with talks that started in November 2025.

[Icelandair's passenger notice](https://www.ruv.is/frettir/innlent/2026-09-16-icelandair-bydur-farthegum-ad-breyta-bokunum-vegna-verkfallsins-487244) still had every Icelandair flight on the schedule, with this contingency: if the walkout starts, all of the airline's scheduled flights from Iceland would be pulled, international and domestic. In September the airline says it typically runs about **110** flights a day and carries about **15,000** passengers a day.

## What this means for travelers

If you have booked **on Icelandair** for **September 23rd or 24th**, those are the dates in the current change offer. Icelandair is letting those passengers try to move the trip, **subject to open seats**. More than **1,000** passengers had already used that offer as of September 17th, according to Icelandair spokesperson Guðni Sigurðsson.

The walkout is **indefinite**. If it starts and runs, disruption can continue past those two days. The change offer as described so far does not cover later dates.

If you are already in Iceland with an Icelandair-operated flight home on those dates, those are the Iceland-origin flights the airline currently says would stop. If you are connecting through Keflavik on an Icelandair-operated flight, the onward or return segment that leaves Iceland is in that same published plan. Inbound Icelandair flights from North America to Keflavik are not in the published stop plan, but the airline has not confirmed they would run; they could still be delayed or canceled. Icelandair has said it will contact passengers if the schedule changes. Keep the email and phone on the reservation current.

If you have not booked yet, the strike risk is on flights operated by Icelandair on those dates. The risk follows the operating flight, not just who sold the ticket. A flight sold by another airline but operated by Icelandair can still be affected, so check your confirmation for "operated by Icelandair." If both the ticket and the operating flight are on a different airline, this specific walkout does not apply to you.

## What to do now

1. If you hold an Icelandair booking for September 23rd or 24th and your dates can move, use [Manage booking](https://www.icelandair.com/support/pre-flight/manage-booking/) or the Icelandair app while seats still exist. Third-party bookings go back to the seller. If another airline sold you a flight operated by Icelandair, contact that ticketing airline for changes.
2. Confirm the contact details on the reservation. Icelandair says it will email if the itinerary changes.
3. If Icelandair cancels your flight, take its [rebooking or refund path](https://www.icelandair.com/support/customer-care/flight-cancellation/) first. Under [EU air passenger rules](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/passenger-rights/air/index_en.htm), you can choose reimbursement or re-routing, plus care (meals, and a hotel if you are stuck overnight). Cash compensation is a separate claim. Icelandair's cancellation page lists strikes broadly as extraordinary circumstances. [EU courts have ruled](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A62020CJ0028) that a legally noticed strike by an airline's own staff over pay, like this one, usually does not count as extraordinary (Case C-28/20, Airhelp v. SAS, 2021). That can keep a compensation claim open for a cancellation tied to this walkout; eligibility still depends on the flight and the facts. Wait for the airline to cancel or rebook before you buy a seat on another carrier, or you may lose that path.

## Bottom line

Friday's talks failed, and the indefinite mechanics walkout is still set for 00:01 on September 23rd unless a deal lands first. Icelandair flights are still operating. If the walkout starts, the airline currently says its scheduled flights from Iceland would stop. If you are booked on Icelandair on the 23rd or 24th, use the change offer if you can, and watch the reservation through Wednesday.
