Colombia’s civil aviation authority is bringing western airports back online after a [USGS-reviewed magnitude 7.4](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us6000tjl2) earthquake on **August 10th, 2026**, but the reopen is staggered and still incomplete. **Matecaña International Airport in Pereira** remains closed to regular commercial flights. Several Coffee Axis and Pacific terminals are flying again under limits, while **Popayán** stays shut for volcanic ash.

Colombia’s government has confirmed at least 273 deaths and 377 people still missing from Monday’s quake, with search-and-rescue still underway.

If you are booked through Pereira, Cartago, Manizales, Armenia, Cali, Quibdó, Buenaventura, or Popayán this week, check your airline before you leave for the airport. National airspace itself is open. The traveler issue is damaged or restricted terminals.

## Which airports are open

Colombia’s [Aeronáutica Civil (Aerocivil)](https://www.aerocivil.gov.co/publicaciones/4619/aeronautica-civil-informa-reapertura-gradual-y-estado-operacional-de-los-aeropuertos-del-pais-tras-evaluaciones-por-sismo/) published an updated airport status after technical inspections. The traveler-facing picture:

**Commercial service resumed**

- **Alfonso Bonilla Aragón (Cali / CLO):** commercial operations back to normal; Aerocivil reported no damage to the passenger terminal or aircraft movement areas.
- **El Caraño (Quibdó):** regular commercial operations resumed as a controlled airport after facility checks.
- **El Edén (Armenia):** commercial service resumed as a controlled airport, but the terminal is temporarily **unavailable as an alternate** for other aircraft because of physical conditions in the building.
- **Gerardo Tovar López (Buenaventura):** commercial operations allowed as an **uncontrolled** airport (flights can run without full tower service).
- **La Nubia (Manizales):** commercial operations allowed as an uncontrolled airport while the control tower is repaired after cracks and broken glass; Aerocivil says the runway and movement area are safe.

**Still restricted or closed**

- **Matecaña (Pereira / PEI):** regular commercial operations remain **suspended**. The airport is limited to state flights, medical flights, search and rescue, and humanitarian missions while crews work on runway cracking, air-traffic communications, and control-tower assessments. A NOTAM covers the restriction.
- **Santa Ana (Cartago):** uncontrolled, reserved for state, emergency, and humanitarian flights only.
- **Guillermo León Valencia (Popayán):** temporary closure for **volcanic ash** in the area (a separate safety hold from the quake damage list).

Aerocivil also says national air navigation systems (communications, surveillance, navigation, weather) are operating normally across Colombian airspace.

## What this means for travelers

Pereira is the main Coffee Axis gateway for many US and Canada itineraries that connect through Bogotá or Medellín. If your ticket shows **PEI**, regular commercial flights are still suspended until Aerocivil and your airline say otherwise. Ground transfers from Cali, Armenia, or Medellín are long, and some roads in the region remain restricted after the quake, so check current conditions before you treat a same-day bus as the backup.

Cali is the clearest western reopen for commercial traffic. Armenia can move passengers again, but it is temporarily unavailable as a diversion airport for other aircraft. Manizales is also flying again as an uncontrolled airport while its tower is repaired. Cartago is not a commercial option right now.

If you are already in western Colombia, follow local authorities on building safety and aftershocks, then handle flights through your airline. If you are still inbound from abroad, confirm the Colombia domestic leg before you fly the long-haul. A canceled PEI connection can leave you stuck in Bogotá or Medellín overnight.

## Airline flexibility windows

Start with **your airline’s rebooking path**, not a same-day DIY ticket on another carrier.

[Avianca](https://ayuda.avianca.com/hc/en-us/articles/54212840825755-Important-information-regarding-disruptions-caused-by-the-earthquake-in-Colombia) expanded flexible options for tickets on **direct or connecting flights through August 30th, 2026**, to or from **Pereira, Armenia, Cali, and Quibdó**. Subject to availability and at no extra charge, published options include:

- Change the travel date through **September 30th, 2026**, with no change fee or fare difference.
- Reroute through other airports inside Colombia (you pay your own ground transport to or from the new city).
- Request a refund for unused flight segments.

Those options apply to all fare types and do not include compensation for missed connections. If you bought through a travel agency, contact the agency. Avianca tells passengers to check status first on [avianca.com](https://www.avianca.com/) or the app.

[LATAM Airlines Colombia](https://www.latamairlines.com/co/es/prensa/comunicados/latam-airlines-colombia-anuncia-flexibilidades-para-pasajeros-afectados-por-terremoto-en-colombia) published flexibility for Pereira, Cali, and Armenia tickets. Wingo also reported its own window. Rules differ by carrier and have moved since the first notices, so open manage booking rather than assuming Avianca’s dates apply.

## What to do now

1. Open your booking and confirm whether the airport code is still operating commercial flights (especially **PEI** vs **CLO** / **AXM** / **MZL**).
2. Use the airline’s published rebooking path first. Keep screenshots of any free-change window shown in manage booking.
3. If you are re-routed to another city, get the airline’s onward plan in writing before you pay for a long private transfer across the Coffee Axis.
4. If you get stuck overnight, keep receipts. Flight delay insurance on the card that paid for the ticket can cover hotel and meals after a long enough delay on many Canadian and US travel cards. Thresholds vary. For the broader checklist, see [Flytrippers on canceled and delayed flights](https://flytrippers.com/most-important-things-related-to-canceled-and-delayed-flights/).

## Bottom line

Aerocivil is reopening western Colombia airports in stages after the August 10th magnitude 7.4 quake near San José del Palmar. Cali is back to normal commercial service, several regional airports are flying with limits, and Pereira’s Matecaña airport is still limited to state, medical, rescue, and humanitarian flights. Check your airline and Aerocivil updates before you head to the airport.
