Record rainfall in parts of Chiba Prefecture cut road and rail access to Tokyo’s Narita International Airport (NRT) on Thursday, August 13th, into Friday morning, August 14th, 2026. An airport spokesperson put the overnight crowd at about **7,000** people across the three terminals. Flights were still scheduled to operate on Friday. Getting to or from the airport was the hard part.

Same summer habit as [O’Hare storm ground stops](/news/chicago-ohare-storm-ground-stops-waivers/) and [Catania ash closures](/news/catania-airport-etna-ash-ferragosto/): check the flight, then check whether the ground side can actually deliver you.

## What happened

Heavy rain hit Chiba hard enough that train and airport-bus services into and out of Narita stopped on Thursday. [NHK](https://www3.nhk.or.jp/nhkworld/en/news/20260814_07/) reported that up to 7,000 travelers stayed overnight, with some people allowed into restricted terminal areas. Airport staff handed out food, water, and sleeping bags.

Parts of Chiba saw more than **360 mm** of rain in 24 hours (about **14 inches**), according to rainfall totals reported Friday. That is an extreme summer dump for the prefecture that sits next to Tokyo and hosts Narita. Highway operator NEXCO East closed sections of major Chiba expressways, which pushed remaining road traffic onto alternate routes.

Narita’s own airport notice flagged the same problem in plain language: [ground transport may experience delays](https://www.narita-airport.jp/en/news/20260813_heavyrain/), and travelers should check the operator running their train, bus, or taxi.

## Scheduled flights, partial ground recovery

An airport spokesperson said Friday flights were scheduled to operate normally. Japan Airlines said some of its flights could still see delays, with cancellations not expected at that point.

That split matters for travelers. The runway can look usable while the Narita Express, Keisei lines, highway buses, and taxi queues are still broken or thinning out slowly.

On Friday, JR East suspended **all Narita Express (N’EX)** limited-express services for the day. Other Chiba JR limited expresses, including Sazanami, Wakashio, and Shiosai, were also out. As of Friday morning updates, some local JR sections had midday or evening restart targets; others had no restart time posted. Keisei Electric Railway restarted the Narita Sky Access Line from the first trains Friday on a **reduced** schedule, while parts of the Keisei Main Line toward the airport stayed disrupted earlier in the morning.

By Friday morning, some train and bus services were running again and people formed long lines to leave. Crowds at the terminals had begun to thin, but access was still messy.

## What this means for travelers

If you were already at Narita overnight, the priority is a reliable way into Tokyo or to your next hotel. Expect queues when rail and bus capacity comes back in pieces.

If you are still trying to **reach** Narita for a Friday departure, leave more time than a normal N’EX or airport-bus pad. A flight that is still listed as on time can become a missed departure if your only surface option is suspended or crawling. Confirm the train or bus before you leave central Tokyo, not only at check-in.

If you land at Narita today, do not assume the usual airport train is waiting. Recheck JR East and Keisei status, then the [Narita access page](https://www.narita-airport.jp/en/access/), before you commit to a connection into the city.

This is peak Obon travel week in Japan, so when service returns it can still feel crowded. Build buffer for bags, immigration, and the next available train rather than a tight city hotel check-in.

## What to do

1. Check **your flight** in the airline app first. Rebooking, delays, and overnight care still start with the carrier that holds the ticket.
2. Recheck ground access the same day: [JR East train information](https://traininfo.jreast.co.jp/train_info/e/index.aspx), [Keisei’s Skyliner English page](https://www.keisei.co.jp/keisei/tetudou/skyliner/us/index.php), and Narita’s [heavy-rain notice](https://www.narita-airport.jp/en/news/20260813_heavyrain/) plus [access guide](https://www.narita-airport.jp/en/access/).
3. If you are stuck overnight at the airport or a nearby hotel, keep receipts for meals and lodging. Card flight-delay insurance on many Canadian and US travel cards can help when the delay or cancellation meets that card’s covered terms. A ground-access miss alone does not automatically qualify. Thresholds vary. For the broader disruption checklist, see [Flytrippers on canceled and delayed flights](https://flytrippers.com/most-important-things-related-to-canceled-and-delayed-flights/).
4. If you can shift a same-day Tokyo hotel night closer to Narita, book that hotel directly. If you need to move an outbound flight while seats remain, use the airline’s rebooking path rather than buying a second ticket first.

## Bottom line

Record Chiba rain stranded about 7,000 travelers at Narita overnight by cutting road and rail access, even while Friday flights were still scheduled. N’EX was suspended for Friday, and other airport links were only partially back. Confirm the flight, then confirm how you will actually get to or from NRT before you leave.
