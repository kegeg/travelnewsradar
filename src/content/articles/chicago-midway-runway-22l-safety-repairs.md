The Chicago Department of Aviation said on October 20th, 2026, that Runway 22L at Midway International Airport (MDW) is closed to arrivals and departures for repairs to a damaged runway safety system. Repairs are targeted for mid-November, and delays or cancellations are possible, so check your airline before you go.

Midway is Chicago's southwest-side airport, separate from O'Hare, which had [thunderstorm ground stops in August](/news/chicago-ohare-storm-ground-stops-waivers/).

## What closed

The [Chicago Department of Aviation](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/doa/provdrs/dbata/news/2026/October/10202026.html), in consultation with the FAA, said the closure is effective immediately. The Engineered Materials Arresting System (EMAS) at the departure end of Runway 22L was damaged by recent aircraft activity, including jet blast.

EMAS is a crushable bed past the runway end, built to stop a plane that overruns. The [FAA](https://www.faa.gov/airports/engineering/incursions_excursions/emas) says a standard system can bring a runway's critical aircraft to a complete stop when it enters the bed at 70 knots (about 80 mph / 130 km/h) or less. Midway's runways are short and sit close to city streets, which is why that bed is there.

CDA said the airport has a four-runway network. How much this slows things down will depend on airline schedules, weather, and especially wind direction, which affects how the remaining runways can be used.

[FAA notices](https://notams.aim.faa.gov/notamSearch/) currently list Runway 22L closed to takeoff and landing from October 19th through November 13th, 2026, with the 22L EMAS listed unserviceable in the same window. CDA's public completion target is still mid-November. That notice window can move.

As of late Tuesday evening, October 20th, the [FAA's Midway status page](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=MDW&p=1) showed no destination-specific delays, and gate-hold, taxi, and airborne delays of 15 minutes or less. That page is not flight-specific. The [national airspace status board](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) had no Midway ground stop.

Southwest is Midway's main airline. Its [travel advisory page](https://www.southwest.com/travel-advisory/) listed no advisories as of Tuesday evening. Look in manage booking rather than assuming a fee-free change window.

## What this means for travelers

If you are flying O'Hare, this runway closure does not apply.

If you have booked to, from, or through Midway between now and mid-November, the airport is open. Runway 22L is closed. Your flight can still run on time, or it can delay or cancel, especially when wind limits which remaining runways the airport can use.

If you connect at Midway, a delay on the inbound can still mean you miss the outbound even when the FAA board looks quiet.

If you are still booking a trip after mid-November, recheck the airline and the Midway status page before you count on the runway being back.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app before you go to the airport.
2. Recheck the [FAA Midway status page](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=MDW&p=1) and the [national airspace status board](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) on the day you travel, especially if Chicago is windy.
3. Look for a travel waiver in manage booking before you pay a change fee. Southwest had no Midway runway advisory posted Tuesday evening.

If the airline cancels your flight, stay on its rebooking path first. Under [DOT refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), you can get a refund if the airline cancels the flight or makes a qualifying significant change or delay and you choose not to travel or reject rebooking. Taking the replacement flight generally ends that refund path. For a practical checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

Midway closed Runway 22L for safety-system repairs, targeted for mid-November. The airport is open. The FAA board showed only light delays Tuesday night. Check your flight if you are booked through MDW.
