Israel’s main gateway, **Ben Gurion Airport (TLV)**, shut check-in and paused landings on **Thursday, August 20th, 2026**, after a same-day staff stoppage. The Israel Airports Authority (IAA) said work disruptions under workers’ committee instructions hit passenger services on one of the summer’s busiest days. Takeoffs and landings began a gradual restart from **3:00 p.m.** local time (8:00 a.m. Eastern) on a rebuilt schedule. Delays and baggage backlogs can continue after the counters reopen.

Another airport labor day after [Schiphol’s short security stoppages](/news/schiphol-security-short-stoppages-ultimatum/) and [Barcelona’s Groundforce walkout](/news/barcelona-el-prat-groundforce-strike/). For many North American tickets, [Air Canada still has Tel Aviv suspended into 2027](/news/air-canada-dubai-tel-aviv-suspension/), so this stoppage mainly hits carriers that are actually flying TLV today.

## What happened

By early afternoon Israel time, check-in counters stopped processing passengers. The IAA told travelers that from **2:00 p.m.** local, air activity would be cut back until landings stopped, with departures leaving gradually against an updated timetable. Several inbound flights already en route turned around and returned to their departure countries rather than waiting for a landing slot.

The authority said the stoppage followed instructions from the workers’ committee. Employees of ground-services companies at the airport were also reported to have halted work. The IAA called for an immediate return to normal operations and said it was ready to seek a labor-court order if the disruptions continued. Transport Minister Miri Regev publicly directed the IAA to petition the labor court and warned of consequences for whoever ordered the halt.

The Histadrut’s transport workers union said it did **not** issue a work-stoppage directive. Union messaging blamed long-running staffing shortages and summer overload, and rejected calling the mess a surprise strike. Workers’ committee chairman Pinchas Idan, described by Israeli outlets as leading the stoppage, said management is overworking staff. For travelers, the sequence was the same either way: check-in closed, landings paused, then a staged restart.

Same-day reporting put Ben Gurion near a peak of about **100,000** passengers. The IAA had separately flagged summer parking pressure and European airspace constraints.

## What this means for travelers

If you were departing TLV on Thursday afternoon, the first hit was closed check-in and a frozen bag drop. If you were inbound, landings paused and some flights already en route returned to their departure countries. After 3:00 p.m. local, the airport began rebuilding the day’s board. A restart still leaves a backlog. Expect longer waits at check-in and baggage claim, plus late departures that can push return legs later the same day or overnight.

If you are still traveling into or out of TLV in the next day or two, keep watching airline status until your airline confirms your flight is operating on the updated schedule. A same-day tight connection through Tel Aviv has little slack when bags and gates are catching up.

People already in the terminal should stay with their airline’s instructions rather than assuming the original departure time still holds. People still heading to the airport should confirm status before they leave for Lod.

## What to do now

1. Check your live booking and the [IAA flight board](https://www.iaa.gov.il/airports/ben-gurion/flight-board/) before you leave for the airport. Use the airline app or contact center for your exact flight, not only the airport board.
2. Follow [IAA notifications](https://www.iaa.gov.il/en/airports/ben-gurion/notifications-and-updates/) for passenger updates. The IAA has advised travelers to stay in direct contact with their airlines on timing changes.
3. If your inbound flight diverted or returned to origin, use your airline’s rebooking and care path first. Do not buy a second ticket until the airline says what it will cover.
4. If you are already ticketed for a later TLV departure this week, turn on airline alerts and recheck the morning you fly. Residual schedule slip is the main near-term risk.

## Bottom line

Ben Gurion closed check-in and paused landings on August 20th during a workers’ committee stoppage. The IAA blamed labor instructions; the Histadrut said it did not order the work stoppage. Flights began restarting from 3:00 p.m. local on a reorganized board. For travelers, the live job is airline status, the IAA board, and patience while Thursday’s backlog clears.
