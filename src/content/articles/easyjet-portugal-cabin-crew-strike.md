easyJet cabin crew based in Portugal started a five-day walkout on **Friday, October 2nd, 2026**. It runs through **Tuesday, October 6th**. The union, SNPVAC, says **443** flights tied to the Lisbon, Porto, and Faro bases are already canceled for that window, about **80%** of those bases' flying. Some easyJet trips to and from Portugal are still operating. If you have booked on easyJet into or out of those airports through Tuesday, check the reservation before you leave for the airport.

Another Europe labor story after [easyJet France cabin crew filed a summer strike notice](/news/easyjet-france-cabin-crew-strike-notice/). This one is already pulling trips, and it overlaps Portugal's Republic Day public holiday on **Monday, October 5th**.

## What the union and easyJet say

[SNPVAC](https://site.snpvac.pt/blog/2026/09/17/tripulantes-de-cabine-da-easyjet-anunciam-greve-para-inicio-de-outubro-e-vespera-do-natal/) is the Portuguese cabin-crew union. In September, easyJet crew at the Lisbon (LIS), Porto (OPO), and Faro (FAO) bases voted to walk out October 2nd through 6th, and again **December 19th through 23rd**. The union points to unstable rosters, pay progression by seniority, and a company agreement still in talks.

On Friday morning, SNPVAC said the 443 cancellations already cover most of the five-day program at those three bases. It also said more than **60** Lisbon flights, more than **30** Porto flights, and **eight** Faro flights were still on the schedule and could still be affected.

easyJet said Thursday it expects to operate about **55%** of its Portugal program during the walkout. It put required minimum services at about **22%** of Portugal-based flying, and said it is also running flights with aircraft and crew from other European bases that are outside this dispute.

Those two percentages are not the same list: the union is counting flights crewed from the Portuguese bases, and the airline is counting all easyJet flying to and from the country. Check your own flight.

SNPVAC says other-base crew are already covering some Portugal flying, including French crew that arrived in Porto on Thursday, and that substituting striking cabin crew that way is illegal. The union also said Friday morning a Porto flight was using local managers. On Wednesday, easyJet told the union those staff were assigned to office duties. easyJet says its operations follow the applicable rules. SNPVAC plans a complaint with Portugal's labor inspectorate plus court action. The court complaint is separate from your ticket.

## What still flies

[Portuguese labor authorities](https://www.dgert.gov.pt/despacho-sm-n-o-51-25-09-easyjet_snpvac-greve-de-3-a-6-de-outubro-e-de-19-a-23-dezembro) set minimum services for the five days. Protected flying includes Lisbon-Funchal (two daily rotations) and Porto-Funchal (one daily rotation), plus some Europe routes and limited Porto Santo links on October 4th and 6th. A protected route can still change. Confirm yours in the booking.

## What this means for travelers

If you have booked **on easyJet** into or out of **Lisbon, Porto, or Faro** between Friday and Tuesday, you are the group that can get hit. Connecting onto an easyJet flight at those airports can get messy too, even if you arrived in Europe on another airline.

If your ticket is not on easyJet, this cabin-crew walkout does not apply to you.

Many US and Canadian trips to Portugal use another airline across the Atlantic, then an easyJet hop inside Europe. The easyJet segment is the one to check for October 2nd through 6th.

If you have booked on easyJet to or from Portugal in December, today's cancellations do not automatically pull those tickets. The next published walkout window is December 19th through 23rd.

## What to do now

1. Open [Manage Bookings](https://www.easyjet.com/en/manage/viewbooking) or the easyJet app and check [flight status](https://www.easyjet.com/en/help/boarding-and-flying/delays-and-cancellations) for your exact flight. If easyJet already canceled it, you can request a free switch, a voucher, or a refund there.
2. If easyJet cancels your flights, use that rebooking or refund path first, including any other easyJet routing it offers. If it cannot get you to your destination within 24 hours, its delays page says you may book another airline, train, bus, or rental car yourself and claim reasonable costs back. Third-party bookings should also go back to the seller.
3. For flights covered by [EU air passenger rights](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/passenger-rights/air/index_en.htm), you can choose reimbursement or re-routing, plus care (meals, and a hotel if you are stuck overnight). Cash compensation is a separate claim. easyJet's [notice of rights](https://www.easyjet.com/en/terms-and-conditions/notice-of-rights-for-flight-delays-and-cancellations) lists strikes among possible extraordinary circumstances. [The Court of Justice of the European Union ruled](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A62020CJ0028) that a legally noticed strike by an airline's own staff over pay usually does not count as extraordinary (Case C-28/20, Airhelp v. SAS, 2021). Eligibility still depends on the flight and the facts.

Keep receipts if you are stranded. Card flight-delay insurance on many Canadian and US travel cards can help when the delay meets that card's terms. For the broader checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

Portugal-based easyJet cabin crew are walking out through Tuesday, October 6th. The union says 443 Lisbon, Porto, and Faro flights are already canceled. Check your booking. Some minimum-service flights, and some trips crewed from other European bases, are still operating.
