Iranian airlines canceled some international flights after a US Treasury aviation-sanctions wind-down ended at 12:01 a.m. Eastern on **Wednesday, September 23rd, 2026**. Iran's Civil Aviation Organization said Baghdad and Muscat would stop those services, and ISNA later reported the UAE had stopped all Iranian-carrier flights from midnight Wednesday into Thursday.

If you booked on an Iranian airline, check that ticket before you go to the airport. If you booked on Emirates, Etihad, Air Canada, or another non-Iranian carrier, use that airline's own advisory.

Another Middle East aviation story after [Dubai and Abu Dhabi delays following a US-Iran strike exchange](/news/dubai-abu-dhabi-flight-delays-us-iran/) and [Air Canada's Dubai and Tel Aviv suspensions](/news/air-canada-dubai-tel-aviv-suspension/).

## What happened

The US-Iran conflict is still underway. On **September 8th, 2026**, the Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control ([OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260908)) designated 27 remaining Iranian airlines and suspended licenses that had allowed overflight payments, bunkering and emergency repairs, and certain foreign-airline flights into Iran on US-origin or US-controlled aircraft. [Iran General License DD](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/936901/download?inline=) kept a wind-down window through **12:01 a.m. EDT on September 23rd**. Mahan Air was already designated in 2011. Iran Air was already under older US aviation sanctions.

Treasury's [September 8th release](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0623) warned that foreign firms fueling, handling, or selling tickets for sanctioned Iranian airlines risk being cut off from the US financial system. Each destination still decides whether to keep handling the flights.

[Iran's Civil Aviation Organization](https://www.tasnimnews.ir/fa/news/1405/06/31/3703763/) said Baghdad airport would stop accepting Iranian flights from midnight on September 23rd, Iran time, and that Muscat would stop accepting Iranian passengers. Officials were talking about moving Baghdad services to Najaf. It told passengers to check with the airline or a travel agency before flying.

[Iran Air said on Tuesday](https://www.tasnimnews.ir/en/news/2026/09/22/3703470/iran-air-says-istanbul-baku-najaf-flights-remain-in-operation) it had not been told to stop Istanbul, Baku, or Najaf flights, and would keep those on the schedule until it got an official notice. Reports later said Georgia and Azerbaijan suspended the services. A Tehran travel agent said Qatar flights had also stopped, and that some Istanbul flights were still scheduled on Wednesday while others were canceled. The agent said they had stopped selling tickets for now because of cancellation risk.

Flight-tracking showed a Mahan Air flight from Tehran landing in Guangzhou after the US wind-down ended. Tehran's international airport board still listed other Turkey and Armenia connections on smaller Iranian airlines, including Varesh, plus Najaf. Dubai was still on that Wednesday board. By Thursday, the same board listed no Dubai, Muscat, or Baghdad flights. Istanbul, China, and Bangkok connections were still listed. ISNA reported that the UAE had stopped all Iranian-carrier flights from midnight Wednesday into Thursday.

## What this means for travelers

If you hold a ticket on Iran Air, Mahan Air, Qeshm Air, Varesh, or another Iranian airline, check that booking today. Baghdad and Muscat are the confirmed stops. ISNA reported the UAE stopped Iranian-carrier flights too, including Dubai. Istanbul and the other mixed routes could still change status.

These Iranian-airline cancellations do not automatically cancel a booking on Emirates, Etihad, Air Canada, or another non-Iranian carrier. If your itinerary still touches Iran or an affected hub, check that carrier's current advisory.

The [US travel advisory for Iran](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iran.html) is **Level 4: Do not travel**. [Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/iran) advises **avoid all travel**, and tells Canadians already there to leave now if they can do so safely. Those pages predate Wednesday's cancellations.

People already in Iran should follow local instructions and the same-day advisory. Canada says some airlines have suspended Iran flying, but commercial flights and overland crossings remain, with limits. Dual Canadian-Iranian citizens can be required to show an Iranian passport and then blocked from leaving. The United States has no embassy in Iran.

## What to do now

1. If you booked on an Iranian airline, confirm the flight with that airline or the agency that sold it before you leave for the airport. That includes Baghdad, Muscat, and Dubai if that is your ticket.
2. If they cancel it, use their rebooking or refund path first, including any reroute they offer. Najaf was the Iraq alternative they were discussing.
3. If you are in Iran and trying to leave, reread [Canada's Iran advice](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/iran) or the [US Iran advisory](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/iran.html) the same day you move. Keep travel documents current.

If you booked on another airline, check that carrier if your routing still goes through Iran or an affected hub.

## Bottom line

Some Iranian-airline international flights stopped after the US aviation wind-down closed, including Baghdad, Muscat, and, according to ISNA, the UAE. Other routes still flew, including Istanbul and China connections. Check the operating airline if that is your ticket.
