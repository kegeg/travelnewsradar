US flights were still being canceled Sunday, two days after the FAA lifted Friday's nationwide ground stop. The agency now says L3Harris router work flooded the old communications network. Check your flight before you go, and use the waiver on your ticket if you want a different day.

This follows [Friday's nationwide ground stop](/news/faa-nationwide-ground-stop/). The command center canceled that stop at 8:16 p.m. Eastern. Aircraft, crews, and bags can still be in the wrong cities.

## What caused Friday's stop?

The FAA's [Sunday Q&A](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/nationwide-ground-stop-questions-and-answers) dates the technical problem at about 3:30 p.m. Eastern Friday. The command center issued the nationwide stop at 3:54 p.m. The FAA's older telecommunications network became unstable when data hit it at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds. That disrupted aircraft communications and radar feeds.

The initial investigation points to two sites: a radar site in Amarillo, Texas, and a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas. L3Harris, the FAA's network provider, was deploying a new router at El Dorado. L3Harris then blocked those two locations, and the network stabilized. The FAA also turned off broadcasting on the new routers, and as a precaution on about 60 similar routers in the system.

Instability showed up at 17 of 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers, which handle planes once they are airborne between airports. It then spread into some terminal radar facilities. The FAA says it has no indication this was a cyberattack. About 3,500 aircraft were already in the air when the stop went in. The FAA says every one of them landed.

Operators canceled about 12,462 flights Friday, including 9,082 by major airlines. The FAA says not all of those were caused by the ground stop. It says the system is stable and operations are returning to normal.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Sunday that airspace would come back to normal Sunday and probably be fully normal Monday, with airplanes and crews still out of position from Friday.

## Sunday operations

As of about 8:16 p.m. Eastern Sunday, [FlightAware](https://www.flightaware.com/live/cancelled/today) showed about 2,100 cancellations within, into, or out of the United States, and about 9,300 US delays. Some of that is ordinary weather. San Francisco was showing an average delay of 140 minutes for low ceilings.

The [FAA status board](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) was not listing a nationwide ground stop.

[Delta said at 11:15 a.m. Eastern Sunday](https://news.delta.com/delta-continues-operational-recovery-following-faa-mandated-nationwide-ground-stop-extends-flexible) that most flights were operating normally, with some delays and cancellations still possible. It asked travelers who can move to go Monday, November 16th, or later. [American said at 12:55 p.m. Central](https://news.aa.com/news/news-details/2026/Update-on-FAAs-nationwide-ground-stop/) that Sunday cancellations were fewer as it kept restoring the operation.

[Air Canada](https://www.aircanada.com/us/en/aco/home/book/travel-news-and-updates/2026/us-ground-stop.html) still says recovery will take time and to check status before heading to the airport. [WestJet](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/get-travel-ready/advisories) still has an Air Traffic Control advisory up for travel November 13th through 15th.

## What this means for travelers

If you fly Sunday night or Monday, check **your flight** in the airline app before you leave. The FAA says it is safe to fly and the nationwide stop is gone. A Monday flight can still be canceled if the airplane or crew is in the wrong city.

Delta and Southwest still cover Monday as original travel. JetBlue and WestJet's posted windows stop at Sunday. American's 21-airport list now runs original travel through November 18th.

If you are still booking a trip later in November, nothing material should change.

Canadian itineraries that go to, from, or through a US airport take the same hit. A Canada departure can still be late if the US airport, the crew, or the airplane was held.

## Airline change windows

Each airline posted its own window. Open the one on your ticket before you pay a fare difference.

| Airline | Original travel | Change window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delta | November 13th to 16th, systemwide | Change by November 23rd, new flight by November 23rd |
| American | November 13th to 18th, 21 airports | Book by November 18th, can travel November 13th to 21st |
| JetBlue | November 13th to 15th, JetBlue flights | Through November 18th, before the original departure |
| Southwest | November 13th to 16th | Same cities, within 5 days of the original date |
| WestJet | November 13th to 15th, listed cities | One-time change or cancel in Manage Trips |

[Delta's waiver](https://www.delta.com/us/en/advisories/other-alerts/air-traffic-control-outage) covers any city on November 13th through 16th, on tickets issued on or before November 15th. Same cabin. The fare difference is waived if you change by November 23rd and the new flight leaves by then. A fare difference can still apply if your original booking class is gone, or if you change the origin or destination. You can cancel for an eCredit.

[American's alert](https://www.aa.com/i18n/travel-info/travel-alerts.jsp), still stamped November 14th, includes Atlanta, Chicago O'Hare, Dallas/Fort Worth, Miami, Los Angeles, JFK, LaGuardia, Newark, and Reagan National. Denver, Seattle, San Francisco, Houston Intercontinental, and Dulles are not on the 21-airport list. The ticket has to have been bought by November 12th. Basic Economy is included, the cities stay the same, and you stay in the same cabin or pay the difference. Changes have to be booked by November 18th. You can cancel and request a refund.

[JetBlue's alert](https://www.jetblue.com/travel-alerts), updated 11:00 a.m. Eastern Sunday, covers JetBlue-operated flights November 13th through 15th booked before November 13th. Fees and the fare difference are waived through November 18th if you change online before the original departure. You can also cancel for a refund to the original form of payment. A group of 10 or more has to call.

[Southwest's advisory](https://www.southwest.com/travel-advisory/) says you can rebook or stand by within five days, between the same cities, without a change in airfare. If Southwest cancels the flight or delays it significantly and you decide not to travel, it says a nonrefundable ticket can be refunded.

WestJet's window is travel November 13th through 15th to a list of US, Mexico, and Caribbean cities. Direct bookings can make a one-time change or cancellation in Manage Trips. Agency and third-party tickets go back to the seller. A Monday WestJet flight to those cities is outside that listed window.

[United's travel alerts page](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/travel/trip-planning/travel-alerts.html) did not show a dedicated ground-stop window Sunday evening. Open manage booking before you pay a fare difference. Air Canada's recovery page does not print a dated change window. Use self-service in the booking if the flight is canceled.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app before you go to the airport.
2. If you want a different flight, use the waiver on your ticket while that window still exists. Deadlines differ by airline.
3. If the airline cancels your flight, stay on its rebooking path first. Keep receipts if you are stuck overnight.

Air traffic control equipment problems in the US generally do not trigger a standalone airline cash payment for a simple delay. Under [DOT refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), a cancellation or a significant delay is a different case if you choose not to travel. Card flight-delay insurance can still cover a hotel or meals. For a checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

The FAA named L3Harris router work in El Dorado, Arkansas, and a radar site in Amarillo as the start of Friday's nationwide ground stop. The nationwide stop ended Friday evening. US flights were still being canceled Sunday. Check your flight if you travel Sunday night or Monday. Delta's window now covers through November 16th, with changes by November 23rd. American's 21-airport list now runs original travel through November 18th, booked by that day.
