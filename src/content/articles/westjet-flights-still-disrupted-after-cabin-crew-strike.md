WestJet and CUPE reached a [tentative agreement on August 3rd, 2026](https://westjet.mediaroom.com/2026-08-03-WestJet-and-CUPE-reach-tentative-agreement,-ending-further-operational-disruption), ending the cabin crew work stoppage and withdrawing strike and lockout notices. Flights have started again, but WestJet says it is still rebuilding the network. Some trips can still be delayed or canceled while aircraft and crews are repositioned.

This follows the [72-hour strike notice](/news/westjet-cabin-crew-72-hour-strike-notice/) and the earlier [free change-or-cancel window](/news/westjet-free-change-cancel-cabin-crew-strike/) while talks were still open. The strike is over, but the schedule is not fully normal yet. CUPE later shared more of [what’s in the tentative deal](/news/westjet-cabin-crew-deal-wage-hike-duty-premium/), including wage and duty-premium terms still waiting on ratification.

## What WestJet is saying now

WestJet’s [travel advisory](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/get-travel-ready/advisories) covers travel **July 30th through August 6th, 2026**. It says a full return to normal will take time, and that some flights may keep getting disrupted while the airline resumes flying and repositions aircraft and crews. WestJet’s banner on that page tells guests to check flight status before leaving for the airport.

The airline’s [guest updates](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/guest-updates) page matches that: operations are recovering, some flights may still be affected, and guests should only go to the airport with a confirmed flight. If a flight is canceled, WestJet says it will work to reaccommodate travelers on the next available flight, including other airlines, or let eligible guests cancel for a refund through [Manage Trips](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/manage).

CUPE says cabin crew are returning to work and that members still need to [ratify the tentative deal](https://cupe.ca/news/westjet-mainline-flight-attendants-reach-tentative-agreement). Contract details go to members before that vote.

## Flexible change and cancel window

For travel **on or before August 6th, 2026**, WestJet says eligible WestJet, WestJet Vacations, Sunwing, and Vacances WestJet Québec guests can make a **one-time** change or cancellation at no additional cost. Changes need to be made at least **two hours** before the original departure. If your flight has already been disrupted and you no longer want to travel, WestJet says you can cancel at any time under that guest-update guidance.

Direct bookings should use Manage Trips. Agency and third-party tickets go back to the seller. Vacation packages go through Sunwing Vacations, WestJet Vacations, or Vacances WestJet Québec.

WestJet’s usual [advisory flexibility rules](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/manage/change-cancel#flexible) still apply: a one-time **$0** change fee, fare difference often waived for a normal date or time shift (not when you change fare type, cabin, or destination), travel usually completed within **60 days** of the original departure, and UltraBasic cancels leaning Travel Bank rather than an automatic cash refund.

## What this means for travelers

The walkout is over, but bookings for the next few days can still get hit. WestJet says restoring the full network will take **several days**. That is the practical problem for anyone flying mainline WestJet through about August 6th: aircraft and crews still have to get back where the schedule needs them.

If your itinerary shows **Operated by WestJet Encore** or a De Havilland Dash 8-400, that segment was outside the cabin-crew dispute. WestJet still warns that connections onto mainline Boeing 737 or 787 flights can be affected, so check the full itinerary, not only the first flight number.

If you are already away from home and a labor-related disruption hits your trip, WestJet notes that under Canada’s Air Passenger Protection Regulations, labor disruptions are treated as outside the airline’s control for standard hotel and meal requirements. WestJet says it is going beyond that for this disruption and will reimburse reasonable required expenses for guests away from home, including accommodation up to **$250 CAD** per night per reservation (taxes and fees included), meals, and ground transport between the hotel and the airport. Keep receipts. WestJet says reimbursement requests must be submitted by **September 30th, 2026**.

## What to do now

1. Check [flight status](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/flights/status) and Manage Trips before you leave for the airport. Only go if the flight is confirmed.
2. If you are traveling on or before August 6th and your plans can move, use the one-time free change or cancel while that window still exists.
3. Watch email and SMS tied to the reservation. Agent bookings should also check with whoever sold the ticket.
4. If WestJet cancels your flight, take the rebooking or refund path in Manage Trips first. Vacation packages contact the vacation provider.
5. If you are stranded away from home, keep itemized receipts and use WestJet’s expense process before the September 30th deadline.

## Bottom line

The cabin crew strike ended with a tentative deal on August 3rd, and flights are coming back. WestJet still expects residual delays and cancellations for several days while it restores the network. Confirm your flight before you head to the airport, and use the August 6th flexibility window if your dates still sit inside it.
