Air Canada has pushed the restart of its Dubai (DXB) and Tel Aviv (TLV) flights again. On its [Middle East travel update](https://www.aircanada.com/ca/en/aco/home/book/travel-news-and-updates/2026/middle-east-travel.html) dated August 4th, 2026, the airline says flights to and from both cities remain suspended and canceled until mid-January 2027. The previous window on that same page pointed to late October.

This sits in the same Middle East aviation and advisory cluster as earlier TNR notes on [US-Lebanon direct flights](/news/us-lebanon-flights-directive/) and the [Aqaba airport alert](/news/aqaba-airport-alert-jordan-travel/). Do not plan on an Air Canada nonstop to Dubai or Tel Aviv this fall.

## What Air Canada changed

Due to the situation in the Middle East, Dubai and Tel Aviv service continues to be suspended, and both routes stay canceled until mid-January 2027.

An earlier update still visible on the same page said both cities remained canceled until October 24th, 2026, inclusive. That is the date travelers had been watching. The August 4th note moves the restart into mid-January next year.

Air Canada does not publish a single guaranteed restart day such as January 15th. “Mid-January 2027” is the wording to use when you talk to the airline or an agent. Treat that mid-January window as a planning marker only; it could move again if the region stays unstable. The page also stacks older suspension windows from spring and early summer, so read the newest dated update first.

## What this means for travelers

If you hold an Air Canada ticket that still shows Dubai or Tel Aviv on a fall or early-winter date, treat that itinerary as canceled or still subject to cancellation under the current advisory. Do not book a new Air Canada itinerary to either city for travel before mid-January 2027 expecting it to operate.

Dubai International Airport itself is still open, with limited and changing service from other airlines. Tel Aviv options also depend on the carrier and the day. This story is only about Air Canada’s own flights.

Canada’s government advice still matters beside the schedule. [Global Affairs Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/united-arab-emirates) currently tells Canadians to avoid non-essential travel to the United Arab Emirates, last updated July 29th, 2026. The same avoid-non-essential framing applies to Israel on Canada’s [Israel and Palestine advice page](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/israel-and-palestine), also updated July 29th. Those pages are separate from Air Canada’s commercial decision; check both before you keep a leisure trip on the books.

If you are already in the UAE or Israel and your Air Canada flight home was canceled, confirm another airline’s flight is operating as scheduled before you go to the airport.

## Flexible booking options: check the live page

Air Canada still lists a flexible booking policy under the March 17th, 2026 update on that Middle East page for planned travel to or from Dubai and Tel Aviv. That block is older than the August 4th suspension extension. As printed there, you can rebook the same destination between September 8th and December 15th, 2026, at no fee on Air Canada-operated flights in the cabin you bought, or change to another Air Canada destination in Europe, the U.K., India, or Africa for travel between May 1st and December 15th, 2026. You can also cancel: Aeroplan members can self-serve an AC Wallet credit, or call the contact center for a Future Travel Credit. Refunds remain subject to fare rules.

Those printed rebook windows end on December 15th, 2026, while the August 4th suspension now runs into mid-January 2027. The older December 15th window may not cover the newer extension. Open your booking on [Air Canada Trips](https://www.aircanada.com/home/ca/en/aco/trips), read the current advisory, and call the contact center if the self-serve path does not match what you need. Not every ticket will qualify the same way.

An older note on the same page tells Air Canada Vacations customers to contact Vacations directly; that channel is separate from the flexible-booking options above.

## What to do now

1. Open your reservation and confirm whether Dubai or Tel Aviv is still showing on an Air Canada flight number.
2. Read the latest dated note on Air Canada’s [Middle East travel update](https://www.aircanada.com/ca/en/aco/home/book/travel-news-and-updates/2026/middle-east-travel.html) before you pay a change fee.
3. If you still need to get there, price another airline or a connection that does not rely on Air Canada DXB or TLV service, and confirm that flight is operating as scheduled before you go to the airport.
4. Recheck Canada’s [UAE](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/united-arab-emirates) and [Israel](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/israel-and-palestine) advice pages, and your insurance wording, before you keep a leisure trip on the books.

## Bottom line

Air Canada’s own advisory now keeps Dubai and Tel Aviv canceled until mid-January 2027, after a prior late-October window. Ticketed travelers should manage the booking against that live page, and anyone still shopping fall or early-winter dates should not count on Air Canada nonstops on either route.
