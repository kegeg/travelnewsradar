Europe’s [Entry/Exit System (EES)](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/ees_en) has been [fully operational](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/entryexit-system-ees-fully-operational-2026-04-10_en) across Schengen external borders since **April 10th, 2026**. For most US and Canadian leisure travelers on a short stay, that means fingerprints, a facial image, and a digital entry record on your first arrival instead of a passport stamp. At busy hubs this summer, that extra step is stacking into long border queues. The separate online pre-travel system, [ETIAS](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en), is still not collecting applications.

Another Europe summer watch item beside [Palma Swissport partial strikes](/news/palma-airport-swissport-strikes/) and the [easyJet France cabin-crew strike notice](/news/easyjet-france-cabin-crew-strike-notice/).

## What EES actually does

EES is a border system at the edge of the Schengen area. It replaced ink stamps for most non-EU nationals coming for short stays. On a first registration, border officers (or kiosks, where set up) take biometrics and store entry and exit data. Later crossings can be faster once your record exists.

It applies when you **enter or leave** the Schengen external border. It does **not** restart every time you fly between Schengen countries.

## What this means for travelers

If you hold a US or Canadian passport and you are arriving in Schengen for a vacation this summer, plan buffer time at passport control on the **first** entry. Industry groups representing airports and airlines have reported waits of **two to five hours** at some busy points during peak waves, plus missed connections when passengers were still stuck in the hall. The European Commission has said most of the roughly **1,500** border crossing points are manageable and has pointed to about **20** difficult spots, often smaller leisure airports that get a short, hard rush of arrivals.

Outcomes vary a lot by airport and time of day.

Once you are inside Schengen, moving between member countries does not put you through EES again. Your exit registration happens when you leave the external border. If you are already in Europe for the summer and staying inside Schengen until you fly home, the live risk is mostly your **arrival day** (already done) and your **departure day**, not every short hop in between.

Border posts can temporarily pause **biometric** collection when waits get extreme, for short windows. The entry/exit record still matters, and EU officials have rejected calls for a full seasonal shutdown of EES. Friends of mine who flew into Paris Charles de Gaulle said the biometric step had been lifted when they arrived because of delays. That can happen under the temporary-pause rules. Do not count on it for your flight.

## My Sofia arrival

I flew into **Sofia Airport** on **June 9th, 2026**, after EES was already fully live. I cleared with an officer: fingerprints, photo, the usual questions. The wait was about **10 to 15 minutes**, and it was basically just our flight in the hall at that moment. It felt quick and efficient.

My girlfriend holds an Italian passport, so she used the EU e-gates and was through in seconds while I did the third-country process. I’m staying inside Schengen for the rest of this stretch and flying out of **Barcelona in mid-August**, so I will not hit EES enrollment again until that exit.

## ETIAS is still not live (and keeps slipping)

ETIAS is the planned online travel authorization for visa-exempt visitors (US, Canada, UK, and many others), similar in idea to a US ESTA. The [official ETIAS site](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en) says the system is **not in operation** and that no applications are being collected. When it does launch, the published adult fee is **€20** (about **US$22** / **CA$30**), with free applications for some age groups, and authorizations meant to last up to three years or until the passport expires.

The timeline has already slipped a lot. After the 2018 EU regulation, ETIAS was first aimed at **2021**, then pushed through **2022**, **2023**, **2024**, and **2025**. The public target most recently associated with the project was the **last quarter of 2026**. It is **widely reported** that another slip into **2027** is likely while EES settles, but the European Commission has not published a firm new start date. Until an official launch announcement lands, do not pay any sites that claim it can sell you an ETIAS today (you don't need it yet).

## What to do now

1. On your first Schengen arrival this trip, build real buffer after landing before any tight connection. Hub choice matters more than it used to.
2. Carry the same passport you will use for the whole trip. EES ties the record to the travel document.
3. Do **not** buy “ETIAS” from random websites. The official system is not taking applications yet.
4. Watch airport and airline notices the week you fly. Temporary biometric pauses happen. Headlines that say EES is fully off for the summer usually overstate what border posts are allowed to do.

EES is live for US and Canadian short-stay arrivals. Budget time at the first Schengen border this summer, expect uneven waits by airport, and ignore anyone selling ETIAS applications before the EU says the system is open.
