King Khalid International Airport (RUH) in Riyadh remains closed Sunday after a Saturday afternoon attack killed 12 people and injured 309, Saudi aviation officials said. If you hold a ticket into or out of Riyadh this weekend, do not leave for the airport until your airline shows the flight as operating.

This follows Thursday's [King Khalid attacks and Delta's Atlanta-Riyadh launch review](/news/delta-atlanta-riyadh-launch-after-airport-attack/), and Wednesday's [Houthi attack on Aden airport](/news/aden-airport-attack-cairo-flight-diverted-jeddah/).

## What Saudi officials confirmed

Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation, in a [Saturday statement](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2697867), said King Khalid International Airport was attacked on Saturday afternoon, October 10th, 2026. Operations were temporarily suspended pending a full damage assessment and safety checks. GACA said the attack came amid continued attacks on civil aviation facilities in the kingdom.

A [Sunday follow-up](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2697996), posted at 1:11 a.m. local time, said the attack killed 12 people: four Saudi citizens, two Bangladeshi residents, and one resident each from the United States, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Palestine, and Sudan. Another 309 people were injured, including some in critical condition. GACA said the injured are receiving care at several hospitals, and that property damage is still being assessed.

Saudi Arabia's [Foreign Ministry](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2697900) told Saudi citizens whose flights to King Khalid were suspended or delayed to contact Saudi missions in the countries where they are now.

GACA has not posted a reopen time. Flight-tracking data on Sunday showed hundreds of Riyadh flights canceled, and many remaining arrivals delayed. Any reported Sunday-night reopen window is unconfirmed until GACA or your airline says the airport is open again.

## What happened earlier this week

GACA [said](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2697151) two attacks hit King Khalid on Thursday, October 8th, killing three Saudi citizens, and that operations had resumed. Saturday's attack shut the airport again.

Yemen has been at war since 2014. The Houthis control much of the country's northwest and have been firing missiles and drones at Saudi airports, energy sites, and shipping. GACA's Saturday statement did not name Saturday's attacker. Houthis claimed earlier strikes this week, and the GCC attributed the October 6th-7th Abha and King Khalid attacks to them.

Riyadh is the capital. King Khalid is the city's main international airport, and the planned arrival airport for [Delta's first Atlanta-Riyadh nonstop](https://news.delta.com/delta-begin-first-nonstop-flight-riyadh-saudi-arabia), still listed from October 23rd.

## What this means for travelers

If you have booked into or out of Riyadh this weekend, the flight is unlikely to operate as scheduled until your airline says otherwise. Do not go to King Khalid based on an old boarding time.

If the airline canceled your flight, use its rebooking path first (app, manage booking, phone, or a partner flight it assigns). Wait for that offer before you buy a second ticket on another carrier.

If you are already in Riyadh, follow local instructions. The airport is closed while officials assess damage. Canada now [advises against all travel](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/saudi-arabia) to King Khalid International Airport. The [US Embassy](https://sa.usembassy.gov/security-alert-u-s-mission-saudi-arabia-october-10-2026/) strongly recommends that US citizens avoid the airport and surrounding areas. The [UK](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia) tells British nationals to avoid King Khalid until the airport is secure, and says the incident happened around 3:00 p.m. local time Saturday.

If you are still deciding whether to travel to Saudi Arabia, those pages now tell you to stay away from the airport itself. Canada still advises against non-essential travel to the country (last updated October 10th, 2026). It also tells Canadians to avoid all travel in Asir, Jazan, and Najran, plus within 30 km (about 19 miles) of the Yemen border. The US remains at Level 3, Reconsider Travel, in an [advisory updated October 10th](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/saudi-arabia.html), with Do Not Travel along the Yemen border. The UK still advises against all but essential travel to Riyadh province, and its page was current on October 11th.

If you hold a Delta ticket on Atlanta-Riyadh from October 23rd, that flight is still on the published schedule and Delta's newsroom has not posted a delay. Watch the booking.

If you do not hold a Riyadh ticket, Saturday's attack does not close other Saudi airports on its own. Check that booking as usual.

## What to do now

1. If you have booked Riyadh this weekend, open the reservation in the airline app or website before you leave for any airport.
2. If the flight is canceled, take the airline's rebooking or refund path first.
3. Recheck the [Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/saudi-arabia), [US](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/saudi-arabia.html), and [UK](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia) pages the same day you travel.
4. If you hold Delta Atlanta-Riyadh from October 23rd, keep watching [Delta's booking](https://www.delta.com/) for a separate notice.

## Bottom line

King Khalid International Airport is still closed Sunday after Saturday's attack killed 12 people and injured 309. GACA has not posted a reopen time. If you hold a Riyadh ticket this weekend, check the airline and stay on its rebooking path.
