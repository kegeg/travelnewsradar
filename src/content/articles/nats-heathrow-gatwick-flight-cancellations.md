A [NATS](https://www.nats.aero/news/technical-issue-causing-some-disruption-to-flight-departures/) flight-processing failure on **Tuesday, September 8th, 2026**, canceled around 1,300 UK flights, according to flight-tracking tallies. Heathrow says Wednesday operations are recovering, with knock-on disruption as airlines put aircraft and crews back in place, so check your airline before you travel.

As with the [Luton lightning shutdown](/news/luton-airport-lightning-strike-flights/) and [Northeast US controller delays](/news/faa-northeast-ground-stops-atc-staffing/), start with your airline's live status.

## What happened

NATS, the UK's main air traffic control provider, posted its first update at **1:46 p.m. British Summer Time** (8:46 a.m. Eastern). It said a technical issue was disrupting some departures. At 3:25 p.m. BST it traced the problem to its flight processing system, the software that turns airline flight-plan data into what controllers use. NATS said airspace stayed open and some flights were still leaving, but recovery would take time.

It implemented a fix at 4:40 p.m. BST and said the system issue was resolved by 7:30 p.m., with controllers working through the backlog. A 10:10 p.m. update called the recovery complex and told passengers to check with their airline for Tuesday night and Wednesday. As of **12:55 p.m. BST Wednesday** (7:55 a.m. Eastern), NATS said operations are stable and a full investigation is coming.

Heathrow, Gatwick, Manchester, and Stansted were among the airports hit on Tuesday. Flight-tracking tallies put UK cancellations around **1,300** that day, with hundreds more recorded early Wednesday, concentrated at Heathrow.

[Heathrow](https://www.heathrow.com/) says Wednesday operations are recovering. Flights are running, but it still expects knock-on disruption while airlines reposition aircraft and crew. It is telling passengers to check the airline and **only travel to the airport if the flight is confirmed**.

## What this means for travelers

If you were booked to, from, or through the UK on Tuesday or Wednesday, especially via Heathrow (LHR), the original departure time on your ticket may already be wrong. Gatwick (LGW) was among the airports hit on Tuesday, so check that booking's live status too. Missed connections, overnight stays, and aircraft sitting in the wrong city are the usual leftovers after an afternoon ATC failure of this size.

If you are flying later this week, NATS says the system issue is resolved. Check **your flight** anyway, because crews and planes can still be in the wrong cities after Tuesday's failure.

If the airline canceled your flight, stay on its rebooking path first (app, manage booking, airport desk, or a partner flight it assigns). Give it the first chance to reroute or refund you.

North American itineraries that connect in London can take the same hit. A canceled Heathrow arrival can strand the onward long-haul even when your US or Canadian airport looks fine.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app or manage booking before you leave for the airport.
2. Recheck [Heathrow's homepage notice](https://www.heathrow.com/) and [Gatwick's flight board](https://www.gatwickairport.com/flights) the same day. Heathrow is explicit: do not go unless the airline confirms the flight is operating.
3. If the airline canceled your flight, take its reaccommodation or refund choice first. If it cannot promptly provide suitable care or an alternative, the CAA says you can arrange reasonable care or travel yourself and claim those costs back.
4. Keep receipts for meals, hotels, and airport transfers if you get stuck overnight. Under UK261 (and EU261 where it applies), airlines still have to provide care and a refund or alternative flight even when air traffic control caused the disruption, but those rights depend on the route and the operating carrier. Fixed cash compensation is a separate question. The [CAA cancellations page](https://caa.co.uk/air-passengers/travel-problems-and-rights/flight-delays-and-cancellations/cancellations/) currently flags this NATS disruption and explains the rules that match your ticket. Air traffic control management decisions are often treated as extraordinary circumstances that can block the fixed payout.
5. If you paid with a card that includes flight-delay insurance, that coverage can still matter for hotel and meal costs after a long enough delay. For a practical disruption checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

NATS resolved Tuesday's flight-processing failure, and it says operations are stable on Wednesday. Heathrow still expects knock-on disruption while aircraft and crews reposition. Confirm your airline status before you travel, and do not go to Heathrow unless that flight is actually operating.
