Yemen's Transport Ministry says Houthis fired ballistic missiles and drones at Aden International Airport (ADE) before dawn on **Wednesday, October 7th, 2026**. One missile landed near the runway minutes before Yemenia flight 603 from Cairo was due to land, so authorities canceled that landing and sent the aircraft to Jeddah.

Another Middle East aviation story after [Dubai and Abu Dhabi flights delayed following US-Iran strikes](/news/dubai-abu-dhabi-flight-delays-us-iran/) and the earlier [Aqaba airport alert in Jordan](/news/aqaba-airport-alert-jordan-travel/).

## What Yemen's Transport Ministry said

The [ministry statement](https://www.mot-ye.net/news/15232), posted at 4:23 a.m. in Aden, said the Houthis targeted the runway and passenger terminal with two ballistic missiles and several explosive-laden drones. Flight 603 was carrying civilian passengers back from Cairo. Staff and passengers were also in the terminal waiting for a Jeddah departure at 3:00 a.m.

Authorities diverted the Cairo flight to Jeddah, moved two aircraft already on the tarmac (one Yemenia, one FlyAden) away from the area, and evacuated the terminal. The ministry said drones were still being tracked over Aden while those steps happened.

The statement did not list casualties.

Yemen has been at war since 2014. Aden is the main international airport for parts of the country outside Houthi control, and the internationally recognized government has used the city as its temporary capital since the Houthis took Sana'a.

## What this means for travelers

If you have booked on Yemenia or FlyAden into or out of Aden today, especially Cairo-Aden or Aden-Jeddah, your flight can move. Yemenia flight 603 did not land at ADE. People already in the Aden terminal for the overnight Jeddah departure were pulled out of the building.

If you were on the diverted aircraft, you are in Jeddah. Use Yemenia's staff or a local office for the rest of your itinerary. Give the airline a chance to reaccommodate you before you buy a second ticket.

If you are already in Yemen and trying to leave, [Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/yemen) has told Canadians to leave if it is safe, since commercial ways out were already extremely limited. Today's strike makes that exit path less predictable.

If you do not hold a Yemenia or FlyAden ticket, this attack does not change a Cairo or Jeddah itinerary on its own. Those airports were not the target of this strike.

Separately, the [Saudi-led coalition](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2695162) said it intercepted and destroyed a Houthi ballistic missile north of Riyadh on Wednesday. That is a different event from the Aden diversion.

## What to do now

1. If you have booked on Yemenia or FlyAden today, contact the airline before you leave for the airport. [Yemenia's website](https://yemenia.com/) currently shows no flights available and points passengers to **+967 1 250 800** or the nearest office. Its [FAQ](https://yemenia.com/faqs) also says published times depend on a daily coalition flying window.
2. If your flight was diverted or canceled, take Yemenia's rebooking path first, including a later Aden arrival or a connection from Jeddah.
3. Recheck [Canada's Yemen advice](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/yemen) (**avoid all travel**, last updated July 23rd, 2026), the [US Level 4 advisory](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/yemen.html) (do not travel; the embassy in Sana'a has been suspended since 2015), and the [UK FCDO page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/yemen) (against all travel; still current October 7th, 2026). Those pages were already at their highest levels before the attack.
4. If you are flying to Cairo or Jeddah on another airline, check that booking as usual.

## Bottom line

A Houthi missile and drone attack on Aden airport before dawn Wednesday forced Yemenia flight 603 from Cairo to divert to Jeddah, and emptied the terminal for a time. If you hold Yemenia or FlyAden tickets today, call the airline. Canada, the US, and the UK already advise against all travel to Yemen.
