Bangkok flooding has delayed baggage and some departures at Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK) since Saturday, September 26th, 2026, into Monday, September 28th. Both Suvarnabhumi and Don Mueang (DMK) remain open. If you fly Bangkok this week, check your flight first, then how you will actually get there, and whether your airline is still waiving change fees.

Same split as [Narita after record Chiba rain](/news/narita-airport-record-rain-stranded-travelers/): aircraft can still move while the ground side is a mess. Several airlines have also opened short fee-free change windows, as they did after [Chicago's storm week](/news/chicago-ohare-storm-ground-stops-waivers/).

## What happened

Flooded roads across Bangkok kept many Thai Airways ground-handling staff from reaching Suvarnabhumi, in Samut Prakan east of the city. Some Thai Airways baggage carts were also out of service. Airport director Kittipong Kittikhachorn said that mix delayed some Thai Airways departures, left arriving passengers waiting longer than usual for bags, and left luggage behind on several flights.

Tourism and Sports Minister Surasak Phancharoenworakul said Sunday night that some passengers had waited four to six hours. Bags from both domestic and international flights were affected. The published airport explanation is about Thai Airways ground handling, not every carrier at Suvarnabhumi.

On Monday, Defence Minister Lt Gen Adul Boonthamcharoen said he would send defence personnel to help with ground operations. Soldiers were then deployed to help move the backlog. The tourism minister said air force staff were already assisting, and Thai Airways had pulled office employees onto the baggage floor and asked the military for trucks to bring stranded staff through flooded areas.

Thai Airways said registered delayed bags would start going out to passengers from Monday afternoon. Deputy Transport Minister Siripong Angkasakulkiat put home or hotel delivery at about three days after registration.

[TAT's Sunday evening update](https://thailand.go.th/guide-book-detail/bangkok-weather-and-travel-update--27-september-2026-1800-hrs) said visitor services were still running, with localized flooding on some roads. Suvarnabhumi and Don Mueang were open. The Airport Rail Link, Red Line, BTS, and MRT were operating. The State Railway Eastern Line was still suspended. Bangkok was declared a disaster zone on Saturday.

## Getting to the airport

[Suvarnabhumi's own flood notice](https://thailand.go.th/guide-book-detail/flooding-situation-and-travel-information-to-suvarnabhumi-airport), carried on Thailand's government portal, says the main airport roads remain open. Skip the Lat Krabang route because of standing water. Use Motorway No. 7 or the Burapha Withi Expressway instead, and allow at least three hours for an international flight and two hours for a domestic one.

TAT's latest update said the Airport Rail Link was operating. Check current service and station access before you count on it. For Don Mueang, the Red Line was also operating. AOT's contact center is 1722, 24 hours.

## What this means for travelers

If you already have a ticket to, from, or through Bangkok, getting to the terminal can take much longer than usual, and Thai Airways bags at Suvarnabhumi have been slow for three days. Officials have warned that clearing the backlog could take another two to three days, depending on flood and road conditions.

If you land at Suvarnabhumi today, do not assume your checked bag will come out on the first carousel cycle. File a delayed-baggage report before you leave if it does not appear, including if you cannot wait. Thai Airways is using a QR-code registration path and a Level 2 baggage office in front of the domestic arrivals exit.

If you are still heading to the airport for a departure, confirm the flight in the airline app, then pick a route that is actually moving. An on-time listing is easy to miss if you sit in flood traffic on Lat Krabang.

People already in Bangkok should separate hotel and neighborhood flooding from the airport problem. TAT's latest update said BTS, MRT, and the Red Line were operating. Check current service before you go.

## Airline change windows

Rules differ by ticket date, airport, and whether a fare difference still applies. Open manage booking or the airline app before you pay a change fee.

Thai Airways expanded its flood measure after a shorter first-weekend window. For tickets issued by September 26th for domestic or international flights to or from Suvarnabhumi between September 26th and October 11th, it is allowing one date change with the change fee waived, on the same route and in the same fare class, if seats remain and travel is completed by October 11th. Changes on or after October 11th follow the ticket's usual rules. A move to a higher cabin during the ticket validity period still has no change fee, but you pay the fare difference. If Thai Airways cancels the flight, it says eligible tickets get a full refund. Confirm those terms in the [Thai Airways](https://www.thaiairways.com/) app or site before you change.

Thai AirAsia (FD), Thai AirAsia X (XJ), Nok Air, Thai Lion Air, and Thai Vietjet posted their own change or credit options for flood-affected Bangkok flying. The first weekend notices generally covered travel around September 26th through 28th. Nok's first Don Mueang note was tighter, centered on September 26th, though later notices have also listed September 26th through 28th. AirAsia and Thai Vietjet windows have been published both as September 26th-28th and as September 26th-30th. Use the airline's current page rather than a Saturday screenshot.

Bangkok Airways said passengers scheduled September 25th through 27th who could not reach the airport or missed a flight because of flooding can change their travel date once, with the change fee waived, on the same route, if seats remain, with the new travel date on or before October 10th, 2026. Or they can take a Travel Voucher valid for one year from the date it is issued. Contact 1771 or +66 2-270-6699 (08:00-20:00 Thailand time) or Facebook Messenger FlyBangkokAir.

A waived change fee does not always waive the fare difference.

## What to do

1. Check **your flight** in the airline app or manage booking before you leave for the airport.
2. Build extra time on the ground. For Suvarnabhumi, skip Lat Krabang, consider the Airport Rail Link, and use the airport's three-hour international / two-hour domestic pad.
3. If a checked bag does not arrive, file the delayed-baggage report at the airport. Thai Airways has a Level 2 office in front of domestic arrivals, 6:30 a.m. to 11:30 p.m., plus QR-code registration. Thai Airways' contact center is +66 2 356 1111.
4. If you want to move dates while a fee-free window is still open, change in the airline's own tool rather than buying a second ticket.
5. If the airline delays or cancels **your** flight, stay on its rebooking path first, including any partner flight it assigns.

Keep receipts for meals and lodging if you are stranded. Card flight-delay insurance on many Canadian and US travel cards can help when the delay meets that card's terms. For the broader checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

TAT's visitor line is 1672. Tourist Police is 1155.

## Bottom line

Suvarnabhumi and Don Mueang remain open, but Bangkok flooding has delayed Thai Airways bags and some departures at Suvarnabhumi for a third day. Soldiers were sent in Monday to help clear luggage. Thai Airways expanded fee-free Suvarnabhumi date changes through October 11th for eligible tickets, and several other Thai airlines have their own short windows. Confirm the flight, the road or rail path, and the airline's current change rules before you go.
