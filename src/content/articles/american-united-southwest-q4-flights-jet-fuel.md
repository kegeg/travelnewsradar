United will not operate some planned December flights after another jump in jet fuel. American may still tweak late-year flying, and Southwest already cut planned 2026 capacity growth about in half, though it says schedule changes so far have been minimal.

Another capacity story after [Air Transat dropped Montreal and Halifax Fort Lauderdale](/news/air-transat-winter-us-fort-lauderdale-cuts/) this summer.

## What the airlines said

The comments came at Morgan Stanley’s Laguna Conference on Wednesday, September 16th, 2026. Executives said people are still booking, including for the holidays. As of publication, there is no fuel-related travel advisory or waiver tied to these comments. There is no published cancellation list, and none of the three named specific routes.

On September 17th, Southwest said schedule changes so far have been minimal, and that further trims were an illustrative point, not a new December cut. |

American CFO Devon May said each 1-cent move in fuel changes the airline’s quarterly costs by about **$10 million**, which is how he got to the $1 billion fourth-quarter figure. CEO Robert Isom said the airline may still tweak December, and that 2027 growth is likely a little slower than it expected a few months ago. American still [put seven new summer 2027 international routes on sale](/news/american-airlines-summer-2027-international-routes/) in late August. That package stays on sale. The new comment is slower overall 2027 growth.

United CFO Michael Leskinen said higher fuel pushed some weaker routes into the red, so those December flights come out. Southwest CFO Tom Doxey said fall revenue has been strong enough that Southwest still expects to hit its third-quarter earnings guide.

## Why fuel is moving the schedule

[US Gulf Coast kerosene-type jet fuel](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DJFUELUSGULF), the Energy Information Administration spot series, closed at **$4.705 a gallon** on September 15th, 2026 (about **$1.24 USD a liter**). A year earlier, the weekly Gulf Coast average was about **$2.06 a gallon**. Prices were already jumpy in 2026, including a spring spike, a midsummer dip, and this latest run-up. American’s $1 figure is the fourth-quarter forward fuel cost versus its July plan.

Airlines have been raising fares through the year to recoup fuel. United still expects to pass the latest jump through, with a lag because so much of the quarter is already sold. The fare on an unchanged ticket stays put. Remaining seats can still get more expensive.

## What this means for travelers

None of the three has published a route list or announced broad cancellations. United has said some planned December flights will not operate, American may still change late-year flying, and Southwest has not announced new December flight removals.

If you already hold an American or United ticket later this year, the risk is a schedule change on a thin route, or an extra daily frequency coming out.

If you are still shopping, leftover seats can get tighter on weaker routes, especially where United is pulling planned December flying. Holiday weeks can feel tighter when airlines stop adding flights they no longer want at these fuel prices.

Canadians connecting on these airlines to, from, or within the United States are covered by the same [US Department of Transportation refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds). A canceled flight, or a significant change you decline, can get you a refund of the unused fare if you do not take the airline’s rebooking, credit, or other substitute. A significant change includes leaving 3 or more hours earlier or arriving 3 or more hours later on a domestic itinerary (6 hours internationally), a different airport, an extra connection, or a cabin downgrade.

## What to do now

1. Open your reservation on [aa.com](https://www.aa.com/), [united.com](https://www.united.com/), or [southwest.com](https://www.southwest.com/) and confirm the October-December flights you already hold. Watch email and the app for a schedule-change notice.
2. If the airline cancels the flight or makes a qualifying significant change, take its rebooking offer first if that still works for the trip. If you do not want to travel on the new option, use the DOT refund path rather than buying a second ticket on your own.
3. As of publication, none of the three has posted a fuel-related waiver tied to these comments. If you want to change dates on your own, your fare rules and the airline’s normal schedule-change policy apply, and a fare difference may still be due.

## Bottom line

United is taking some planned December flights off the table after jet fuel jumped again, while American may still tweak late-year flying. Southwest cut planned 2026 growth about in half, and says schedule changes so far have been minimal. Check the booking you already have, and plan holiday trips around the flights that are still in the reservation.
