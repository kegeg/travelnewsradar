Another Europe airport labor watch after [Barcelona’s Groundforce walkout](/news/barcelona-el-prat-groundforce-strike/) and [Palma’s Swissport strike days](/news/palma-airport-swissport-strikes/). At **Amsterdam Airport Schiphol (AMS)**, unions **CNV** and **FNV** say security staff will begin **short work stoppages** and **work-to-rule** after a noon **August 19th, 2026** ultimatum to the airport and its three security firms expired without the concrete commitments the unions wanted.

The dispute is about passenger screening at Schiphol. Flights are not automatically canceled. Unions flag **longer security queues** as the main traveler risk, especially if stoppages land in busy windows at the end of the summer peak.

## What the unions called

[CNV’s August 19th statement](https://www.cnv.nl/nieuws/schiphol-laat-ultimatum-verlopen-acties-beveiligers-volgen/) says the noon deadline passed and employers made no immediate, concrete commitments to cut the workload. The first phase is **kortdurende werkonderbrekingen** (short work stoppages) and **stiptheidsacties** (work-to-rule: following rules and processes as written, which can slow throughput). FNV had set the same [August 19th noon deadline](https://www.fnv.nl/nieuwsbericht/algemeen-nieuws/2026/08/beveiligers-schiphol-komen-in-actie-als-er-niet-sn) when it published the ultimatum on August 12th.

Exact stoppage times are **not** posted as a fixed calendar. CNV says each action will be announced only a few hours ahead. If employers still do not move, the unions say longer strikes remain possible later. That next step is still only a union warning. There is no published multi-day walkout schedule yet.

The ultimatum was directed at Schiphol and the three security companies under the current concession: **Securitas**, **I-SEC**, and **Trigion**.

## Why the dispute started

Unions say workload spiked after Schiphol cut security providers from **five to three** in the spring of 2026. Staff moved onto new employers, new rosters, and tighter break and leave patterns. High absenteeism then piled more work onto people still on the floor. Schiphol itself reported [longer security waits on May 18th](https://nieuws.schiphol.nl/wachtrijen-security/) during the start of that new setup, and pointed to understaffing and IT issues that day.

CNV and FNV’s demands focus on breaks that actually work with walk times to rest areas, fairer part-time rostering, at least two free days after five workdays, more leave that gets approved, lighter shift-leader loads, enough female officers for screening lanes, faster toilet relief, and a heat protocol.

## What Schiphol says

A Schiphol spokesperson said on August 19th that the move to actions is unfortunate, that the airport takes security staff concerns seriously, and that Schiphol and the security firms remain ready to keep talking. The airport also said it is already working on concrete improvements for five of the unions’ eight demands, and that it will try to limit impact on travelers. That response was reported the same afternoon the ultimatum expired; Schiphol had not yet posted a matching newsroom note at draft time.

Watch [Schiphol’s newsroom](https://nieuws.schiphol.nl/), the Schiphol app, and your airline for live updates when an action window is announced.

## What this means for travelers

If you fly out of AMS in the coming days, plan for **possible longer security lines** when a short stoppage or work-to-rule window is called. CNV negotiator André Quist has said the short stoppages can mean somewhat longer queues toward the end of the busy summer period. The live risk is time through screening.

Connecting passengers who re-clear security at Schiphol feel this first. Originating passengers with a late arrival at the terminal feel it next. A same-day tight connection through AMS has less slack if screening slows.

## What to do now

1. Leave extra time for security. Schiphol’s May 18th guidance during the reorganization was **two hours** before Schengen flights and **three hours** before non-Schengen flights for check-in and security. That was historical advice for a busy screening day. Follow the current arrival window your airline and Schiphol show for your flight, and add buffer if AMS is under active labor pressure.
2. Turn on airline alerts and check the Schiphol app the morning you fly. Stoppages can be posted only hours ahead.
3. Finish online check-in when offered, and keep cabin bags simple so you spend less time at the belt if a lane slows.
4. If your flight is delayed or canceled, use your airline’s rebooking and care path first.

## Bottom line

Schiphol security staff, through CNV and FNV, are starting short work stoppages and work-to-rule after the August 19th noon ultimatum. Expect possible longer screening queues on short notice, watch live airport and airline updates, and leave extra time. If talks stay stuck, longer strike days could follow later.
