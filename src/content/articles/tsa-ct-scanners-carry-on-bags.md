TSA is still installing computed tomography (CT) scanners at US airport checkpoints. In those lanes you can usually leave laptops and travel-size liquids packed, but the entry tunnel is slightly smaller than the older X-ray machines. Some oversized or overstuffed rolling carry-ons will not fit, and TSA recommends checking large carry-on items with the airline.

Related TSA checkpoint reading: [Gateside visitor passes](/news/tsa-gateside-precheck-without-ticket/) and [Tennessee licenses not scanning](/news/tennessee-drivers-licenses-not-scanning-tsa/).

## What is changing at the checkpoint

[CT machines](https://www.tsa.gov/computed-tomography) make a 3-D image of a carry-on that officers can rotate on a screen. TSA says that clearer picture helps find explosives and other threats, and it cuts the number of bags that have to be opened by hand.

In a CT lane, travelers can leave laptops and other electronics in the bag. Recent airport install notes also say you do not need to pull out travel-size 3-1-1 liquids. Every carry-on item, including roller bags, still goes in a bin instead of straight onto the belt.

The nationwide rollout is not complete, so equipment still varies by checkpoint. TSA's [standard screening page](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening) still tells people in ordinary X-ray lanes to take out electronics larger than a cell phone. TSA's CT explainer still describes keeping 3-1-1 liquids in the bag as a future goal. A [July 13th, 2026 Juneau install note](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2026/07/13/tsa-installs-new-computed-tomography-screening-technology-juneau) already tells CT-lane passengers to leave those liquids packed.

The [3-1-1 liquids rule](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/liquids-rule) itself has not changed. Containers still have to be 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less, in a quart-sized bag. CT only changes whether you take that pouch out.

As of **April 2025**, TSA [told Congress](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/testimony/2025/05/20/oversight-hearing-transportation-security-administration) it had **968** CT machines at **255** of **432** commercial US airports, with nationwide completion not expected until **2043**. Installs have kept going. Juneau got two units in July 2026. [Newark Terminal B](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2024/06/25/tsa-checkpoints-newark-liberty-international-airport-now-equipped) got 11 CT scanners in June 2024.

TSA's [March 18th, 2022 Analogic awards](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2022/03/18/tsa-awards-7812-million-procure-additional-ct-x-ray-scanners-airport) for base and full-size systems were worth up to **$781.2 million**. That was one buy. TSA announced additional awards of **up to $1.3 billion** on [April 12th, 2023](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2023/04/12/tsa-awards-13-billion-procure-additional-ct-x-ray-scanners-airport), with Analogic, IDSS Holdings, and Smiths Detection supplying different unit sizes.

## Will your carry-on fit

TSA has said the same physical limit for years: CT units have a slightly smaller entry tunnel, and not all larger carry-on bags will fit. A [Burlington checkpoint note](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2022/10/06/new-tsa-checkpoint-opens-state-art-technology-burlington) tells travelers not to force larger items into the tunnel and to ask a TSA officer for help. TSA also recommends checking large carry-on items with the airline.

Analogic, the vendor on that 2022 base and full-size award, lists a ConneCT tunnel of **24.4 by 16.5 inches** (about 62 by 42 cm) in its [product datasheet](https://www.analogic.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Analogic_ConneCT_Datasheet.pdf). Smiths Detection and IDSS also supply CT units. TSA has not published one nationwide tunnel size for every model.

TSA does not set cabin-bag dimensions. [Those still vary by airline](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/frequently-asked-questions). Some larger bags may still not fit a CT tunnel, including bags that already meet the airline's size limit.

If it will not go in, ask the officer instead of shoving it. TSA's published advice for large carry-ons is to check them with the airline. That can mean walking back to the ticket counter, and it can mean a checked-bag fee if your fare does not include one.

## What this means for travelers

If you fly with a normally packed bag that already meets your airline's carry-on limit, nothing material should change. In a CT lane you may skip taking out the laptop and the liquids pouch. Keep the same airport buffer you already use, and listen for the officer, because some airports still run mixed machines.

If you fly with an expandable spinner packed full, or a bag that already barely fits the airline sizer, that is the bag TSA is talking about. You can still bring it to the checkpoint. If it will not fit the tunnel, you may have to check it.

If you are departing a Canadian, European, or other non-US airport and you do not go through TSA, this does not apply. It applies when you use a US checkpoint, including Canadians connecting or originating in the United States.

## What to do

1. Pack the bag in the shape you will carry it. Leave the expansion zipper closed if you can.
2. If the bag will not go into the machine, ask a TSA officer. Do not force it.
3. Keep following 3-1-1, and follow the officer at your lane for what comes out of the bag.

## Bottom line

TSA CT scanners are in hundreds of US airports and more are still being installed. They can keep laptops and travel-size liquids in the bag. They also have a smaller tunnel, and TSA recommends checking large carry-ons that will not fit. If yours is oversized or overstuffed, ask the officer and be ready to check it.
