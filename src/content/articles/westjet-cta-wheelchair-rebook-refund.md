The Canadian Transportation Agency ordered WestJet to fly, rebook on another airline, or refund travelers who give at least 21 days' notice that a medically necessary mobility aid cannot collapse below 35 inches (89 cm). The reported release date is **October 30th, 2026**. The Agency has not posted the decision on its public rulings pages.

If you do not travel with a large wheelchair or similar aid, nothing about your WestJet booking changes. Air Canada separately [updated its mobility-aid allowance in August](/news/air-canada-three-mobility-aids/). The WestJet duty comes from an Agency order after a complaint.

## What the Agency ordered

The case started with a 2023 attempt by Noa Fleischer, who uses a power wheelchair, to book WestJet. The chair would not fit through the cargo door of the airline's Boeing 737s. ARCH Disability Law Centre represented her. The Agency found an ongoing undue barrier on most of WestJet's aircraft and ordered a change in how WestJet must treat her and other passengers in the same situation, without awarding her cash.

With at least 21 days' notice, the reported order gives WestJet three ways to meet that duty:

- fly the original WestJet flight, if the passenger shows the mobility aid can safely be tilted to fit through the baggage-compartment door
- book another airline on the same route, or a comparable one, at a comparable time, and pay any fare difference in the same class of service
- refund the full itinerary if no suitable alternative exists

A pricier seat on a competitor in the same cabin should not come out of your pocket. An upgrade to a premium cabin is not covered. A refund is for the ticket, not hotels, tours, or other prepaid trip costs.

The Agency rejected WestJet's argument that booking passengers on other airlines would put it at a competitive disadvantage. WestJet did not provide figures on how many passengers would need the accommodation, or what it would cost, so the evidence did not show undue hardship.

The reported order gives WestJet until **December 14th, 2026**, to confirm to the Agency that the accommodation is available, and until **March 11th, 2027**, to file new policies and proof that staff have been trained.

The order does not guarantee an accessible flight in every case. If WestJet refuses, it is expected to explain why and, where appropriate, provide supporting documents. Following the order does not, on its own, trigger cash inconvenience pay under the [Air Passenger Protection Regulations](https://protection-passager-passenger.otc-cta.gc.ca/en/refunds-and-compensation/flight-delays-cancellations-rebooking-refunds-compensation).

## What WestJet's pages still say

WestJet's [wheelchairs and mobility aids page](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/accessible-travel/wheelchair-mobility-aids) still lists a **35-inch (89 cm)** height cap and a **300-pound (136 kg)** weight cap on Boeing 737s, which are most of the fleet. Limits are higher on the Dash-8-400 and on some 787 loadings. The airline currently says powered devices will not be tilted or placed on their side during loading, transport, or unloading.

Recheck that page before you fly. The 737 height figure is the same 35 inches the Agency used. The live page has not yet been rewritten around the new duty.

[Federal rules](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2019-244/section-44.html) still let a large airline refuse a mobility aid if the cargo door or compartment is too small, if airworthiness is at risk, or if the aid exceeds the lift or ramp. Those are minimums. The Agency can still order more when a barrier is undue. That is the framework in the [2023 Air Canada cargo-door decision](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/ruling/123-at-a-2023) that the Federal Court of Appeal later upheld, and it is the framework here.

WestJet's [accessible-travel page](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/accessible-travel) already says that if it refuses you or your mobility aid, it will give a written reason within 10 days, try to book another WestJet trip at the lesser fare, and refund the ticket if it cannot accommodate you on any WestJet flight. The Agency's order adds booking another airline, at WestJet's cost, as a required option.

In a separate complaint, the Agency has already told WestJet to remove the 300-pound weight cap from most of its aircraft by **June 30th, 2027**. The live WestJet page still shows that cap. Cargo-door height is this October order, with December 2026 and March 2027 dates. The weight cap has the 2027 summer deadline.

## What this means for travelers

If you fly WestJet with a power wheelchair or other medically necessary aid that will not collapse below 35 inches, book at least 21 days out and tell WestJet in writing. Ask which of the three options it will use, and keep the answer.

If your trip is already inside 21 days, this extra duty may not apply. The usual [48-hour mobility-aid notice](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/helpful-tips-accessible-transportation-large-wheelchairs-and-mobility-aids) still applies, as do WestJet's own refusal, other-WestJet-flight, and refund paths.

Companions on the same booking should make sure the mobility-aid details sit on the reservation so a second airline, if you are moved, has them. Travelers on WestJet flights to, from, or within Canada are under the same large-airline accessibility rules. US Department of Transportation rules stay as they are.

## What to do

1. Measure the aid at its most collapsed height and its weight. Compare those numbers with [WestJet's live aircraft limits](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/accessible-travel/wheelchair-mobility-aids).
2. Book at least 21 days before departure. Add the mobility aid when you book or in Manage trips, then confirm in writing. WestJet's accessibility line is 1-888-937-8538.
3. Ask in writing whether WestJet will fly you as booked, rebook you on another airline at its cost, or refund you.
4. If it refuses, keep the written reason, which is due within 10 days. Try the airline first, then [file an air travel complaint with the Agency](https://formulaires-forms.otc-cta.gc.ca/en/air-travel-complaint).
5. If you are rebooked on another airline, confirm that carrier has your mobility-aid and battery details at least 48 hours before departure.

## Bottom line

WestJet must fly, rebook on another airline, or refund travelers who give 21 days' notice that a medically necessary mobility aid cannot collapse below 35 inches. The 737 height cap is still on WestJet's website. Get the airline's plan in writing before you lock the rest of the trip.
