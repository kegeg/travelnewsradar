On **July 30th, 2026**, the union representing WestJet cabin crew served a **72-hour strike notice**. WestJet responded with a matching **lockout notice**. If there is no deal, a work stoppage could begin as early as **August 2nd at 00:01 Mountain Time**. Flights are still operating today, but WestJet says it is preparing a reduced schedule and may start changing or canceling trips before that deadline.

This is the hard next step after [WestJet’s free change-or-cancel window for travel July 30th through August 4th](/news/westjet-free-change-cancel-cabin-crew-strike/). That flexibility is still live. After the stoppage, [recovery delays and cancellations](/news/westjet-flights-still-disrupted-after-cabin-crew-strike/) still hit trips while the network rebuilt.

## What changed today

[WestJet’s travel advisory](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/get-travel-ready/advisories) now states that a 72-hour notice has been issued in the cabin-crew talks. In a [July 30th statement](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/westjet-responds-to-union-s-72-hour-strike-notification-and-issues-lockout-notification-817144459.html), the airline said it answered the union’s strike notice with a lockout notice, that talks continue, and that the notices alone do not ground the airline.

The [WestJet Component](https://wjcomponent.ca/jobaction) of CUPE Local 8125 confirms the same timeline on its job-action page: the union issued the 72-hour strike notice, WestJet issued the lockout notice, and a legal strike or lockout is possible as of August 2nd.

About **4,400** mainline flight attendants voted **99.4%** yes for a strike mandate on **July 15th, 2026**, after a **97.3%** turnout, according to [CUPE](https://cupe.ca/news/westjet-cabin-crew-overwhelmingly-vote-yes-strike-action). Pay for ground duties has been a central sticking point in public messaging from both sides.

## Who would be affected

A cabin-crew stoppage would hit **mainline** WestJet flying. WestJet has said **WestJet Encore** trips on Q400 aircraft and **codeshare** flights operated by partner airlines would not be impacted by this dispute. Check your confirmation for the operating carrier and aircraft type if your booking shows a WestJet flight number.

A mainline Boeing 737 trip is the booking that carries the cabin-crew risk. Encore and partner-operated flights on the same confirmation email do not.

## What this means for travelers

If you have booked on WestJet **on or between July 30th and August 4th**, you can still make a **one-time** change or cancellation with **no change/cancel fee** under the [current advisory](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/get-travel-ready/advisories). Use [Manage Trips](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/manage) if you booked direct. Vacation packages go through WestJet Vacations, Sunwing Vacations, or Vacances WestJet Québec. Third-party bookings go back to the seller.

For direct bookings, WestJet’s usual advisory rules still apply: fare difference is often waived for a normal date or time shift, but not when you change fare type, cabin, or destination; travel usually must finish within **60 days** of the original departure; and UltraBasic cancels lean Travel Bank rather than an automatic cash refund. Changes and cancels need to be more than **2 hours** before departure.

If your dates sit outside July 30th–August 4th, this advisory does not give you the same free change. Watch for a wider window if the dispute runs longer.

Expect schedule changes before August 2nd. WestJet says it will manage changes and cancellations so guests hear in advance, and that impacted guests will be refunded or reaccommodated as applicable. Direct bookings should get email updates; agent and online-travel-agency bookings should check with whoever sold the ticket.

## What to do now

1. Confirm whether your trip is **mainline WestJet**, Encore, or a partner-operated codeshare.
2. If you are in the July 30th–August 4th window and your plans can move, use the free change/cancel tool **before** seats on backup options disappear.
3. Turn on WestJet alerts, watch [advisories](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/get-travel-ready/advisories), and check your reservation email through early August.
4. If WestJet cancels your flight, take the rebooking or refund path it offers first, then compare it with [Canada’s air passenger rights](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/interruptions) for labor disruptions.
5. Leave buffer around cruises, weddings, and same-day connections if you stay on the original dates.

## Bottom line

The 72-hour notices are live. A stoppage can start August 2nd at 00:01 MT if talks fail, and WestJet is already preparing a thinner schedule. Use the free change window if you can, and treat mainline bookings in the next few days as at risk until a deal lands or WestJet confirms your flight.
