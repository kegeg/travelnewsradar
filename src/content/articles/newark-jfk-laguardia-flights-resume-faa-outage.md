Flights are moving again Tuesday at Newark (EWR), JFK, LaGuardia (LGA), and Philadelphia (PHL) after Monday's FAA equipment outage at the Philadelphia Terminal Radar Approach Control, or TRACON. The live FAA airport boards show only light delays at those hubs this morning, but leftover cancellations and airline rebooking windows still matter if you fly today.

This is the same check-your-booking habit as the [UK NATS failure](/news/nats-heathrow-gatwick-flight-cancellations/) and [August's Northeast storm delays](/news/faa-northeast-ground-stops-atc-staffing/). Philadelphia TRACON also handles Newark arrivals, which is why a failure there can stop Newark even when Newark's own tower is fine.

## What happened

On Monday, September 21st, 2026, a primary communications circuit failed at Philadelphia TRACON. When the system switched to backup, the FAA found that a Verizon fiber-optic line in New Jersey had already been cut. New Jersey Transit said a construction crew on one of its projects severed the cable. Verizon said contractors dug it up and that its own cables were properly placed.

The FAA paused arrivals into Philadelphia, Teterboro (TEB), and Newark because of frequency problems at that facility, then expanded restrictions to JFK and LaGuardia. Flight-tracking data showed no commercial landings at Newark from about 9:40 a.m. until just after 5:20 p.m. Eastern.

Flight-tracking tallies put Newark cancellations in the hundreds Monday, including more than half of scheduled departures, with more than 600 flights into and out of the airport canceled by evening. Philadelphia had more than 200 cancellations. United's Newark hub saw the worst of it. Diversions also pushed extra traffic toward Boston Logan (BOS).

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Monday evening that the telecom lines had been repaired and that airport operations in the Northeast were resuming, while delays would take time to clear. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said the failed primary circuit had been due for replacement.

## What this means for travelers

If you were booked to, from, or through Newark, JFK, LaGuardia, Philadelphia, or Teterboro on Monday or Tuesday, check **your flight** before you leave for the airport. The equipment is working again. Aircraft, crews, and connecting passengers can still be in the wrong cities after a day with almost no Newark landings.

If you fly those airports later this week, the FAA is not showing ground stops or delay programs at Newark, JFK, LaGuardia, or Philadelphia this morning. It is keeping extra spacing on New York and Philadelphia arrivals through the morning hours. Boston's current delay program is runway construction, not the Philadelphia outage, though Boston is still on [Delta's waiver](https://www.delta.com/us/en/advisories/other-alerts/northeast-air-traffic-control-equipment-outage) because of Monday's overflow.

If you are still booking a trip next week, nothing material should change.

Canadian itineraries that connect at Newark, JFK, or Philadelphia take the same hit as a US domestic ticket. A canceled Newark arrival can strand the onward flight even when your Canadian airport looks fine.

## Airline waivers still in play

[American's travel alerts](https://www.aa.com/i18n/travel-info/travel-alerts.jsp) include an FAA equipment-outage waiver for Newark and Philadelphia, current as of September 21st:

- Tickets bought on or before September 20th, 2026
- Original travel September 21st through 22nd, 2026
- New travel September 21st through 24th, 2026
- Changes must be booked by **September 22nd, 2026**
- Same origin and destination, or you can move a Philadelphia ticket to Baltimore (BWI), Washington Dulles (IAD), or Reagan National (DCA), or a Newark ticket to JFK or LaGuardia
- Same cabin, including Basic Economy; a cabin change can cost a fare difference

That American rebook deadline is today.

[Delta's Northeast ATC outage waiver](https://www.delta.com/us/en/advisories/other-alerts/northeast-air-traffic-control-equipment-outage) covers travel to, from, or through Boston, Newark, and Philadelphia on September 21st and 22nd. Tickets must have been issued on or before September 21st. You can change the flight on or before September 25th, and the new departure must also be on or before September 25th. JFK and LaGuardia are not on that Delta city list.

If you hold a United ticket through Newark or Philadelphia, open manage booking on [United's travel alerts page](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/travel/trip-planning/travel-alerts.html) before you pay a change fee. Confirm any ATC-delay window that still applies to your ticket. Other airlines that touch those airports may have short windows too. Rules differ by purchase date, original travel date, cabin, and city pair.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app or manage booking before you go to the airport.
2. Recheck the [FAA National Airspace System status](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) and your airport's FAA page ([Newark](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=EWR&p=1), [JFK](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=JFK&p=1), [LaGuardia](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=LGA&p=1), [Philadelphia](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=PHL&p=1)) for a ground stop that may not show in the app yet.
3. Look for a travel waiver in manage booking before you pay a change fee. If you hold an American ticket that qualifies, rebook today.
4. If the airline canceled your flight, stay on its rebooking path first. Keep receipts for meals or hotels if you get stuck overnight.

Air traffic control equipment failures in the US generally do not trigger a standalone airline cash payment for a simple delay. Under [DOT refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), a cancellation or significant delay is a different conversation if you choose not to travel. If you paid with a card that includes flight-delay insurance, that coverage can still matter for hotel and meal costs after a long enough delay. For a practical checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

Monday's Philadelphia TRACON circuit and fiber failure stopped Northeast arrivals for hours, with Newark hit hardest. The lines are repaired, and Tuesday morning FAA boards at Newark, JFK, LaGuardia, and Philadelphia look quiet. Check your flight anyway, and use a waiver in manage booking before you pay change fees. American's Newark and Philadelphia window closes today.
