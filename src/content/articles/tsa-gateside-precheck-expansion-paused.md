[Gateside by TSA PreCheck](https://www.tsa.gov/precheck/gateside-tsa-precheck) is back to the original **13 US airports** after a short expansion that briefly put other airports in the application portal. Ticketed passengers keep using the checkpoint as usual. If you wanted a Gateside pass at Denver, Norfolk, Piedmont Triad, or any airport outside that original list, you cannot apply there right now.

Related TSA reading: [how Gateside launched](/news/tsa-gateside-precheck-without-ticket/) and [TSA CT scanners](/news/tsa-ct-scanners-carry-on-bags/).

## What changed

TSA [announced Gateside](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2026/09/09/tsa-launches-gateside-tsa-precheck-bill-and-hillary-clinton-national) on **Wednesday, September 9th, 2026**. It is a free, one-day authorization for eligible TSA PreCheck members and other Known Traveler Number (KTN) holders who are not flying. You apply online **one to three days** before the visit and, if approved, use a PreCheck lane with an [acceptable ID](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/identification).

Earlier this month, additional airports started showing up in that portal. Denver International, Norfolk International (ORF) in Virginia, and Piedmont Triad International (PTI) in North Carolina have all said they were on the wider list. Norfolk and Piedmont Triad both date the add to **October 2nd, 2026**.

TSA then took those extra airports back off. Denver International said TSA "recently returned Gateside by TSA PreCheck operations to the original 13 participating airports, at least for the time being." Piedmont Triad's executive director, Kevin Baker, said the airport was glad to be included and hopes the program returns. Norfolk International said ORF later disappeared from the airport list in the application, and that TSA told the airport the wider program is on hold aside from the original 13.

TSA has said it is assessing participation and how the program is affecting operations at those 13 airports. It has not given a date for adding airports again. The live program page still names the original 13 and still says more airports will be added in the coming months. TSA has not posted a separate rollback press release.

## What this means for travelers

If you are flying, nothing material should change. You still need a boarding pass, and you still go through screening.

If you have a KTN and you want a Gateside pass at one of those 13 airports, Gateside is still the PreCheck-lane path. You still have to apply in advance and get approved. There is no same-day walk-up.

If your airport is not on that list, including Denver, Norfolk, and Piedmont Triad, you cannot get a Gateside pass there right now. Meet people in public areas such as baggage claim. Airlines can still issue a limited **gate pass** in narrow cases, for example accompanying a minor, a person with disabilities, or a military member. Those visitors use standard screening lanes.

Caroline's family is in Michigan, and **Detroit Metro (DTW)** is still on the remaining list. We have flown DTW visiting them. If we wanted to walk someone to a gate there, that is still the airport where Gateside would actually work.

## What to do

Most travelers can ignore this. If you wanted a Gateside pass:

1. Confirm the airport is still on the [live list](https://www.tsa.gov/precheck/gateside-tsa-precheck), then apply **one to three days** ahead at TSA.gov/gateside with a KTN that matches your PreCheck record.
2. If the airport is not listed, plan to meet in public areas. Extra airports may not come back in time for a trip you already have.
3. If you already applied for an airport that dropped off the list, check the portal and the live airport list before you leave. TSA says approval status can change.

## Bottom line

Gateside still runs at the original 13 US airports. A brief expansion to other airports, including Denver, Norfolk, and Piedmont Triad, has been paused while TSA assesses the program. Ticketed flights are unchanged. Check the live airport list before you apply.
