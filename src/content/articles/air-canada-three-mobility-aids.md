Air Canada announced on August 28th, 2026, that customers can travel with up to three mobility aids at no charge. The update also covers adaptive sports equipment and some transfer devices, adds a free cargo option inside Canada when a mobility aid will not fit the same plane, and lets travelers track loading and unloading in the Air Canada app.

If you do not fly with a mobility aid or adaptive sports gear, nothing about your booking changes. This is a policy clarification for people who do.

## What Air Canada changed

In its [August 28th newsroom release](https://www.aircanada.com/media/air-canada-takes-additional-measures-to-make-travel-easier-for-customers-travelling-with-mobility-aids/), Air Canada says customers can now travel with up to three mobility aids at no charge. Eligible items can be any combination of:

- personal mobility aids
- adaptive sports equipment
- a personal or patient lift or similar transfer device

The airline frames the change as giving clearer, more consistent choices for leisure trips, medical travel, work, and adapted sport. The Canadian Paralympic Committee and Air Canada’s Accessibility Advisory Committee both welcomed the defined policy in the same release.

If a mobility aid is too large to travel on the same aircraft as the customer, Air Canada Cargo will transport it within Canada at no additional charge. The free cargo path covers Canadian itineraries only.

Air Canada also says the app can show loading and unloading status for a mobility aid, so you can confirm the device was put on the flight and taken off on arrival. The same announcement points to other accessibility work already underway under its Accessibility Plan: specialized lift equipment at airports across Canada, handling-procedure reviews, and staff training.

For day-of and booking detail, use Air Canada’s traveler pages on [mobility aids and assistance](https://www.aircanada.com/ca/en/aco/home/plan/accessibility/mobility-aids-and-assistance.html) and [special items / adaptive sports equipment](https://www.aircanada.com/ca/en/aco/home/plan/baggage/special-items.html), not only the press release.

## What this means for travelers

For travelers who rely on a wheelchair, scooter, walker, or other aid, and for adaptive athletes who also need sports gear or a lift, the useful part is a clearer free allowance of up to three items and a stated backup when the device will not fit your flight inside Canada. Aircraft size and battery rules still matter: oversized power chairs and lithium batteries often need advance coordination even when the airline does not charge a baggage fee.

Companions and caregivers on the same booking should confirm that requested accessibility services appear in the booking confirmation so gate and ramp staff have the right details. Travelers without mobility or adaptive-sports equipment can ignore this. The announcement covers mobility-related equipment and does not announce changes to ordinary baggage rules.

## What to do if you travel with mobility aids

1. Contact Air Canada Accessibility Services to add or reconfirm your mobility-aid and accessibility needs well before departure, then recheck the live [mobility aids](https://www.aircanada.com/ca/en/aco/home/plan/accessibility/mobility-aids-and-assistance.html) page for packing and battery instructions.
2. If a device may not fit your aircraft on a Canadian itinerary, ask early whether the free Air Canada Cargo path applies to that aid and those airports.
3. On travel day, use the Air Canada app’s mobility-aid tracking to confirm loading before departure and unloading on arrival, and keep removable parts in your cabin bag when the airline’s guidance says to.

## Bottom line

Air Canada’s August 28th policy lets eligible customers bring up to three complimentary mobility aids or adaptive sports equipment, adds free domestic cargo when an aid will not fit the same plane, and includes app tracking for load and unload status. Check the live accessibility pages for your specific equipment before you fly.
