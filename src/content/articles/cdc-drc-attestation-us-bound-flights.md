Starting at **11:59 p.m. EST on November 3rd, 2026**, every person boarding an international flight to or through the United States has to sign a [CDC attestation](https://www.cdc.gov/port-health/legal-authorities/attestation-order.html) that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the previous 21 days. If you were not in the DRC, this is a form at check-in or boarding. If you were, including a connection that never left a DRC airport, you cannot board until Day 22.

Canada already bars many foreign nationals after recent DRC time. We [covered those rules in July](/news/canada-ebola-border-rules-drc-travel/). CDC issued the attestation order on October 22nd and amended it on October 28th. The [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/27/2026-21828/requirement-for-attestation-for-all-airline-or-other-aircraft-passengers-arriving-in-the-united) posts the requirement through **11:59 p.m. EST on December 18th, 2026**, unless CDC changes it sooner. The signed Order text also names December 17th, so recheck the CDC page before you fly.

## Who has to sign

The form applies to **all** air passengers leaving a foreign country for the United States: US citizens, US nationals, and lawful permanent residents (green-card holders). It applies at pre-clearance airports, on commercial flights, and on general aviation and private aircraft. A connection through the United States still needs it. It does not apply at land borders or seaports.

Each passenger needs their own attestation. A parent, guardian, or other authorized person signs for a traveler under 18, or for someone who cannot complete it because of a disability.

Airlines have to collect a paper signature, an authenticated digital signature, or an electronic checkbox before boarding, and they have to refuse anyone who does not complete it unless CDC has issued an exemption. [Qantas](https://www.qantas.com/en-ca/travel-information/updates) is already telling passengers traveling to or via the United States on or after November 3rd to print the CDC form. Other airlines may put it in online check-in instead. CDC posted a fillable PDF on the order page.

Willfully submitting a false statement can mean criminal fines and imprisonment under 18 U.S.C. § 1001.

## What counts as being in the DRC

CDC counts any physical presence in the DRC in those 21 days: by air, land, or sea. Connecting through a DRC airport counts even if you stayed on the plane.

The clock uses calendar days. **Day 0** is the day you leave the DRC. **Day 1** is the first full calendar day outside the country. You may board a US-bound flight beginning on **Day 22**.

[CDC’s traveler page](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/returning-travelers.html) already says that people who were in the DRC in those 21 days, **including US citizens and nationals**, will not be allowed to board flights with US destinations. The November 3rd attestation is how airlines are supposed to confirm that on every inbound flight, not only on itineraries that start in Africa.

## What this means for travelers

If you were not in the DRC, Uganda, or South Sudan in the past 21 days, **nothing material should change for the trip** besides the form. That includes a Toronto-New York hop and a London-Miami connection. You still have to sign.

If someone on the booking was in the DRC, they cannot board a US-bound flight until Day 22, and that can strand the rest of the itinerary even when the other passengers can sign.

| Last 21 days | Attestation from November 3rd | US-bound boarding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not in the DRC, Uganda, or South Sudan | Sign that you were not in the DRC | Yes |
| In the DRC, including an airside connection | You cannot truthfully sign | No, including US citizens. Earliest boarding is Day 22 |
| In Uganda or South Sudan, not the DRC | Sign (you were not in the DRC) | US citizens and nationals: yes, through IAD, ATL, or JFK for screening. Everyone else, including green-card holders: no until Day 22 |

Uganda and South Sudan sit on a [separate CDC entry order](https://www.cdc.gov/port-health/legal-authorities/evdorder.html), last continued on October 11th. The [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/15/2026-21036/order-under-sections-362-and-365-of-the-public-health-service-act-continuing-the-suspension-of-the) for that continuation runs through **4:59 p.m. EDT on November 10th, 2026**. CDC has extended that order several times already. Recheck it close to travel.

Canada’s [current border measures](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/diseases/ebola/border-measures.html) are posted through **November 27th, 2026**. Foreign nationals who were in the DRC in the past 21 days generally cannot enter, board, or transit Canada, unless they meet an exemption on that page. Foreign nationals after Uganda or South Sudan time can enter and then quarantine for 21 days. Canadian citizens and permanent residents can still enter after any of those three countries, with a health assessment and 21-day quarantine.

Caroline and I are still hoping to go to South Sudan and Uganda later this year if the Ebola situation does not get worse. We have a trip deposit and are holding the long-haul tickets. I am Canadian, and she is American. If we never set foot in the DRC, the new form is something we can sign. A Kinshasa connection would count. After Uganda or South Sudan, I still could not enter the United States on a Canadian passport while those foreign-national rules are in force. She could, through Dulles, Atlanta, or JFK, with screening.

## What to do

1. If you were not in the DRC in the last 21 days, complete the attestation when the airline asks. Print the CDC PDF if your airline is collecting paper. Otherwise watch for a checkbox in check-in.
2. If you were in the DRC, do not count on boarding a US-bound flight before Day 22. Call the airline now if you already hold a ticket inside that window.
3. If Uganda or South Sudan is on your recent itinerary, recheck the CDC traveler page and Canada’s border-measures page before you fly. Those posted end dates have moved before.

## Bottom line

From 11:59 p.m. EST on November 3rd, every passenger on an international flight into or through the United States has to attest they were not in the DRC in the past 21 days. Most trips that never touched the DRC only add a signature at boarding. Recent DRC time, including an airside connection, blocks boarding for everyone, including US citizens, until Day 22.
