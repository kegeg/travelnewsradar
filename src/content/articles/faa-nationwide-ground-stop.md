The FAA held departing flights at US airports Friday afternoon after communication problems at multiple air route traffic control centers, then canceled the nationwide ground stop at 8:16 p.m. Eastern. It has not published a cause. Check your airline before you leave if you are flying Friday night or Saturday.

Friday's order covered departures nationwide. September's FAA equipment failure stopped [Newark, JFK, LaGuardia, and Philadelphia](/news/newark-jfk-laguardia-flights-resume-faa-outage/).

## What happened

The FAA's command center [issued the order at 3:54 p.m. Eastern](https://www.fly.faa.gov/ois/oisedit/adv_oisedit_dis?day=11132026&number=111). It cited extraordinary circumstances and safety, said every airport was ground-stopped, and told departing flights to remain stopped until further notice. Inbound international flights were to be handled case by case. In a 4:10 p.m. Central update, [American said](https://news.aa.com/news/news-details/2026/Update-on-FAAs-nationwide-ground-stop/) flights already in the air could continue to their destinations.

These centers handle planes once they are airborne between airports. The [FAA's statement](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-statement-nationwide-ground-stop), which it labeled preliminary, says communication issues hit multiple centers and technicians are investigating. A [5:46 p.m. Eastern advisory](https://www.fly.faa.gov/ois/oisedit/adv_oisedit_dis?day=11132026&number=120) named the reason as a communications failure. It kept the stop in effect and said flights could leave, as those centers recovered, only when the command center cleared them.

At 8:16 p.m. Eastern the command center [canceled the nationwide ground stop](https://www.fly.faa.gov/ois/oisedit/adv_oisedit_dis?day=11142026&number=5). That notice gives no cause. The FAA newsroom page was still the original preliminary statement.

The last nationwide US ground stop was in January 2023, when the FAA's notice-to-airmen system failed. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told reporters Friday evening there was no indication yet of an attack.

## What this means for travelers

If you were booked on a US flight Friday, or you use a US airport Saturday morning, check **your flight** in the airline app before you go. Departures can be released again. Airplanes and crews can still be in the wrong cities, and a Saturday flight can still be canceled.

As of 8:47 p.m. Eastern, the [FAA status board](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) was not listing a nationwide ground stop. San Francisco was showing an average delay of 138 minutes for wind, through 2:59 a.m. Boston was showing about 26 minutes for volume, through 9:59 p.m., and San Diego about 34 minutes, through 12:59 a.m. The forecast list still included possible ground stops or delay programs at JFK, Philadelphia, Newark, and Las Vegas. The nationwide stop is the one canceled at 8:16 p.m.

[Global Affairs](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/united-states) added the ground stop to its US page at 5:21 p.m. Eastern, before the cancellation. The page still says to expect disruptions and to contact your airline. The risk level is still "Take normal security precautions."

[WestJet's advisory page](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/travel-info/advisories) listed no advisories Friday evening. [Air Canada Vacations'](https://vacations.aircanada.com/en/plan-your-trip/travel-info/travel-advisories) November 13th note said flights from Canada to the US, and flights between Canada and the Caribbean or South America that cross US airspace, may be affected. Check the airline on the ticket. A Canada departure can still be late if the US airport, the crew, or the airplane was held.

If you are still booking a trip later this month, nothing material should change.

## Airline change windows

Each airline posted its own window. Open the one on your ticket before you pay a fare difference.

| Airline | Original travel | Change window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delta | November 13th to 14th, systemwide | Change by November 21st, new flight by November 21st |
| American | November 13th to 14th, 21 airports | New trip November 13th to 17th, book by November 14th |
| JetBlue | November 13th, JetBlue flights | Through November 18th, before the original departure |
| Southwest | November 13th to 16th | Same cities, within 5 days of the original date |

[Delta's waiver](https://www.delta.com/us/en/advisories/other-alerts/air-traffic-control-outage) covers any city on November 13th and 14th, on tickets issued on or before November 13th. Same cabin. Delta says the fare difference is waived if you change by November 21st and the new flight leaves by then. The same page says a fare difference can still apply if your original booking class is gone, or if you change the origin or destination. You can cancel for an eCredit.

[American's alert](https://www.aa.com/i18n/travel-info/travel-alerts.jsp) includes Atlanta, Chicago O'Hare, Dallas/Fort Worth, Miami, Los Angeles, JFK, LaGuardia, Newark, and Reagan National. Denver, Seattle, San Francisco, Houston Intercontinental, and Dulles are not on the list. The ticket has to have been bought by November 12th. Basic Economy is included, the cities stay the same, and you stay in the same cabin or pay the difference.

[JetBlue's alert](https://www.jetblue.com/travel-alerts), timestamped 7:40 p.m. Eastern, still said JetBlue flights that night, other than Europe-bound departures, were being canceled. That line is earlier than the 8:16 p.m. cancellation, so check the app. The waiver covers JetBlue flights on November 13th booked before that day. Fees and the fare difference are waived through November 18th if you change online before the original departure time. A group of 10 or more has to call.

[Southwest's advisory](https://www.southwest.com/travel-advisory/) says you can rebook or stand by within five days, between the same cities, without a change in airfare. Those options were posted to start at 7:30 p.m. Central. If Southwest cancels the flight or delays it significantly and you decide not to travel, it says a nonrefundable ticket can be refunded.

[United's travel alerts page](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/travel/trip-planning/travel-alerts.html) did not show a ground-stop window in the text available Friday evening. Open manage booking before you pay a fare difference.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app before you go to the airport.
2. If you want a different flight, use the waiver on your ticket. Delta, American, JetBlue, and Southwest posted different deadlines.
3. If the airline cancels your flight, stay on its rebooking path first. Keep receipts if you are stuck overnight.

Air traffic control equipment problems in the US generally do not trigger a standalone airline cash payment for a simple delay. Under [DOT refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), a cancellation or a significant delay is a different case if you choose not to travel. Card flight-delay insurance can still cover a hotel or meals. For a checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

The FAA held US departures from 3:54 p.m. to 8:16 p.m. Eastern on Friday after a communications failure at air route traffic control centers, then canceled the nationwide ground stop. It has not published a cause. Check your flight if you are traveling Friday night or Saturday. Delta's same-cabin window runs through November 21st. American's list is 21 airports, and that change has to be booked by November 14th.
