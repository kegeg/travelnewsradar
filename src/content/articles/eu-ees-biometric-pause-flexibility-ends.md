Europe’s [Entry/Exit System (EES)](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/ees_en) has been [fully operational](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/entryexit-system-ees-fully-operational-2026-04-10_en) since **April 10th, 2026**. The temporary rule that let border posts pause fingerprint and facial-image capture when queues got extreme is now running out, so short-stay US and Canadian travelers should plan for normal EES biometric checks again at busy Schengen external borders once that window closes. Under [Regulation (EU) 2025/1534](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/1534/oj), that post-rollout pause window lasts **90 days** after the progressive start ended, then can auto-extend by **60 days** if biometric coverage during the progressive start stayed under **80%**. Industry and border briefings put the resulting calendar end at **September 6th, 2026**. As of September 1st, the European Commission has not published a traveler-facing decision extending that flexibility.

This is a follow-up to our earlier piece on [EES summer border queues](/news/eu-ees-border-queues/). EES itself is not being switched off. What ends is the extra room member states had to skip **biometrics** for short bursts at overcrowded crossings.

## What the pause rule actually allowed

After full rollout, member states could **partially** suspend EES operations at a named border crossing when traffic made waiting times excessive. In practice that meant skipping biometric registration (fingerprints and the facial image) for up to **six hours** at a time, while still collecting the other entry/exit data. It was a short congestion valve for biometric capture only.

[Airports and airlines](https://aci-europe.org/media-room/610-open-letter-to-president-of-the-european-commission-on-the-schengen-entry-exit-system-ees.html) (ACI EUROPE, Airlines for Europe, and IATA) told the Commission in late June that countries had already used that flexibility heavily, that waits still hit multi-hour peaks at some posts, and that they wanted both broader summer relief and a permanent exceptional-circumstances mechanism from September. Brussels has already pushed back on a full seasonal EES shutdown. As of September 1st, it has not published a replacement timetable for the expiring biometric-pause window.

Nine Schengen countries, including France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Greece, Portugal, Belgium, Malta, and Switzerland, have also asked to keep a congestion opt-out past early September. That request is real. A public yes/no from the Commission is not published yet.

## What this means for travelers

If you are a short-stay US or Canadian visitor subject to EES and you cross a Schengen **external** border after the pause window closes, plan as if that temporary biometric skip is gone at busy booths and kiosks. First registration still means fingerprints and a facial image, plus the digital entry or exit record that replaced ink stamps. Later crossings usually verify your existing biometrics and can be faster, but exit days and first arrivals remain the slow points. Residence-permit holders, long-stay visa holders, and some other exempt travelers stay outside the normal short-stay EES path.

Nothing about this deadline changes who needs EES. EU/Schengen citizens stay on their own lanes. The story is about whether jammed halls can still skip the biometric step for people who are in scope.

Airport outcomes will still vary by hub and time of day. Some posts ran smoothly all summer. Others leaned on the pause tool when banks of arrivals stacked up. After the window closes, those posts lose that legal shortcut unless Brussels extends it.

Private comments attributed to EU Transport Commissioner Apostolos Tzitzikostas about keeping some flexibility after September 6th have circulated in August reporting. Those reports stay **unconfirmed**. Until a Commission decision is published, plan on normal EES biometric checks again.

## I’ve already been through EES once

I enrolled at **Sofia Airport** on **June 9th, 2026**: fingerprints, photo, the usual questions, about **10 to 15 minutes** with basically just our flight in the hall. Caroline holds an Italian passport, so she used the EU e-gates. I later left Schengen through **Barcelona in mid-August**, so my exit is already on the digital record. If I fly back into Schengen after September 6th, I still expect a check, just not a brand-new first-time enrollment slog. First-timers and anyone whose biometrics were skipped during a pause may still hit the slower path.

## What to do now

1. If the scheduled flexibility ends after **September 6th, 2026** and no extension is announced, build real buffer at passport control on or after **September 7th**, especially at big hubs and leisure airports where many flights land close together. Do not count on a biometric pause to save a tight connection.
2. Before you fly, check the Commission’s [official EES page](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/ees_en) for any published extension, and watch airport and airline notices for the week you travel.
3. Keep using the same passport for the whole trip. EES ties the record to the travel document.

EES stays live. The summer-style biometric pause window is the piece with a hard date on it. Until Brussels says otherwise, plan your September Schengen arrival or exit without counting on a biometric pause: first-time registration if you do not have a file yet, verification if you already do.
