Canada's [Competition Bureau](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2026/10/proposed-air-canada-united-airlines-arrangement-would-harm-competition-on-key-canada-us-routes.html) says a proposed Air Canada-United expansion would likely raise fares on eight of 10 Canada-US routes where the two airlines still have to set prices on their own. Tickets you already hold do not change. The Minister of Transport still has to decide whether to allow the deal.

Air Canada also [dropped Billy Bishop-Boston and four other US routes](/news/air-canada-ends-billy-bishop-boston-us-routes/) in current filings.

## The 10 routes

Air Canada and United already run a transborder joint venture, in place since July 2022. On most Canada-US flying, they can coordinate fares, schedules, and revenue. A [2012 consent agreement](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/review-proposed-air-canada-united-airlines-arrangement) still blocks that coordination on 10 city pairs:

| Route | Who flies the nonstop now | Bureau fare finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ottawa-Washington | Air Canada and United | Among the largest predicted increases |
| Vancouver-San Francisco | Air Canada, United, and Flair | Among the largest predicted increases |
| Montreal-Washington | Air Canada and United | Among the largest predicted increases |
| Toronto-Denver | Air Canada and United | Among the largest predicted increases |
| Toronto-Houston | Air Canada and United | Among the largest predicted increases |
| Toronto-San Francisco | Air Canada, United, and Porter | Material increase predicted |
| Montreal-Chicago | Air Canada, United, and American | Material increase predicted |
| Toronto-Washington | Air Canada, United, Porter, and American | Material increase predicted |
| Ottawa-New York | Porter (largest share) and United | No material increase predicted |
| Toronto-Cleveland | Air Canada | No material increase predicted |

Toronto in that list means Pearson or Billy Bishop. Washington means National, Dulles, or Baltimore. New York means Newark, JFK, or LaGuardia. Houston means Bush or Hobby. San Francisco means SFO or Oakland. Chicago means O'Hare or Midway.

The Bureau's 2025 passenger shares put Air Canada and United at 100% of nonstop traffic on five of the 10 routes: Toronto-Cleveland, Montreal-Washington, Toronto-Denver, Toronto-Houston, and Ottawa-Washington. On Toronto-Cleveland, Air Canada is already the only airline operating the nonstop. On Ottawa-New York, Air Canada does not operate a nonstop; Porter carries most passengers, and United the rest. WestJet no longer had a Vancouver-San Francisco nonstop in the 2026 data the Bureau reviewed. Flair is still on that route.

The Bureau simulated what would happen if the two could coordinate on these routes. It found materially higher prices on eight of them, resulting in millions of dollars in consumer harm annually. It did not find a material increase on Ottawa-New York or Toronto-Cleveland, where only one of the two operates the nonstop today even though both can sell tickets.

Calgary-Houston is not in the 10. Restrictions there are already suspended under the 2012 agreement, with monitoring that can bring them back if the market changes.

## Who decides on the deal

The Minister of Transport decides whether to authorize the expansion. On June 18th, 2026, Air Canada and United asked Transport Canada to approve an amended joint venture that would add these 10 routes. If the Minister authorizes that, the airlines have said they would then ask the Competition Tribunal to lift the 2012 consent agreement. Until both of those steps happen, they still cannot coordinate fares on these city pairs.

[Transport Canada opened a public-interest review](https://www.canada.ca/en/transport-canada/news/2026/10/transport-canada-evaluating-a-proposed-update-of-the-joint-venture-between-air-canada-and-united-airlines.html) on October 16th, 2026. Canadians can comment through [November 15th, 2026](https://consultations.tc.canada.ca/en/consultation/public-interest-assessment-proposed-update-joint-venture-between-air-canada-and-3). The Bureau's report, dated October 16th and published October 28th, goes to the Minister as part of that review. The Minister can authorize the deal, authorize it with conditions, or refuse it.

On October 16th, Air Canada [said](https://www.aircanada.com/media/air-canada-expands-canada-us-connectivity-with-new-summer-2027-routes-increasing-options-for-travellers-under-its-transborder-joint-business-agreement-with-united-airlines/) the current joint venture has supported 20 new Canada-US routes since 2022, more than 4.3 million passengers flying the two together, and more than 1,200 connecting itineraries. That same release announced separate summer 2027 adds, including Toronto-Louisville, Montreal-Indianapolis, and Edmonton-Newark. Those new routes are not the 10 city pairs in the Bureau report.

The Bureau also said connecting one-stop itineraries are not a close substitute for these nonstops, and that new airline entry on these routes is unlikely to be timely or large enough to hold fares down if Air Canada and United could set them together.

## What this means for travelers

Nothing material should change for a trip you already booked.

If you are shopping one of the 10 routes, Air Canada and United still publish separate fares today on the city pairs where both operate. Porter, American, or Flair still sell some of those nonstops. If the Minister later allows the expansion and the Tribunal lifts the 2012 order, the two could coordinate prices on these routes the way they already do on most other Canada-US flying.

Travelers on Ottawa-Washington, Vancouver-San Francisco, Montreal-Washington, Toronto-Denver, and Toronto-Houston are in the Bureau's highest-increase group. Ottawa-New York and Toronto-Cleveland are the two routes where the Bureau did not predict a material fare jump.

## What to do

1. If you already hold a ticket on one of these routes, keep it.
2. If you are still booking, compare Air Canada, United, and any other nonstop on that city pair. Those fares are still set separately.
3. If you want to comment, Transport Canada's consultation is open to Canadians through **November 15th, 2026**.

## Bottom line

The Competition Bureau says letting Air Canada and United coordinate on 10 Canada-US routes would likely raise fares on eight of them, with the largest predicted increases on Ottawa-Washington, Vancouver-San Francisco, Montreal-Washington, Toronto-Denver, and Toronto-Houston. The report does not approve the expansion or change a ticket you already hold. The Minister of Transport still decides.
