A Delta Air Lines Los Angeles-Tampa flight diverted to Fort Myers on Saturday evening, August 29th, 2026, in thunderstorms around Tampa. After landing, some passengers say crew told them to collect checked bags at carousel 7. Those who left the secure area could not get back through TSA. The same aircraft later continued to Tampa with passengers who had stayed behind TSA, and with checked bags belonging to people left behind.

Weather diversions happen often during Florida summers. The part that matters for travelers is what you do the moment the door opens at the wrong airport.

## What happened

Delta Flight 749 was scheduled from Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) to Tampa International Airport (TPA) on Saturday, with a published morning departure at **10:26 a.m. PDT** and an evening Tampa arrival. Thunderstorms around Tampa led the crew to divert south to Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW) in Fort Myers.

Flight-status data for that date shows the LAX-Tampa service and a Fort Myers-Tampa continuation on the same flight number after the diversion. Separate flight-tracking accounts put the Fort Myers landing around **7:09 p.m.** local, a later departure from RSW around **9:14 p.m.**, and a Tampa arrival around **9:36 p.m.** The hop from Fort Myers to Tampa is short in the air, but it is still a full reboard with bags and security rules on the ground.

Passenger Randi Hipper posted that she was on the LAX-Tampa leg after connecting from Hong Kong, that a flight attendant told the cabin to go to baggage claim carousel 7, and that she was among the first off the plane. She said some travelers waited near the gate while she and others went to baggage claim. By the time the continuation was clear, she said TSA had closed for the night on her side of the airport, so she could not reboard. She also said her checked bag left on the aircraft for Tampa.

That baggage-claim instruction is a **passenger report**. Delta has not published a matching public statement confirming the wording of the announcement. Hipper said several people near the front of the cabin followed the same carousel-number instruction. The exact PA wording stays unconfirmed until Delta or another primary source settles it. The diversion itself, the Fort Myers stop, and the later continuation to Tampa are the solid operational facts.

## What this means for travelers

If you were on Delta 749 Saturday, the split outcome is the story: stay inside security and you could finish on the same aircraft; leave for baggage claim and you could be stuck overnight in Fort Myers while your bag continues without you.

As on [O’Hare storm days](/news/chicago-ohare-storm-ground-stops-waivers/) and [wildfire-smoke slowdowns](/news/canadian-wildfire-smoke-us-canada-flights/), confirm the plan for **your** flight before you walk toward the exits.

Fort Myers is roughly a **two-and-a-half-hour** drive from Tampa in normal traffic (about **225 km / 140 miles** by road). That is recoverable with a rental car, ride-hail, or a friend, but it is expensive and messy after a long connecting day, especially with no bag.

Under [Delta’s U.S. Contract of Carriage](https://www.delta.com/us/en/legal/contract-of-carriage-dgr), weather is treated as force majeure. Hotel and amenity language for long delays generally applies when the interruption is **not** force majeure, so a weather diversion does not trigger Delta’s published hotel-voucher amenities. Delta also lists nearby airports where it will furnish ground transport after a diversion. The Tampa group includes Orlando, Daytona Beach, Melbourne, and Sarasota Bradenton. **Fort Myers is not on that list.** Delta may still arrange ground transportation at its discretion, and it still says it will transport a passenger to the ticketed destination on its next available flight with space. A refund path exists if you choose not to continue under the published significant-change rules.

[DOT’s Fly Rights guidance](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/fly-rights) is blunt on domestic weather: airlines are not required by federal rule to pay for hotels or meals when the delay is outside their control. Each carrier’s own policy decides amenities. [Delta’s delayed-or-canceled page](https://www.delta.com/us/en/change-cancel/delayed-or-canceled-flight) normally excludes weather delays and alternative transportation to the final destination from reimbursement. If you leave the secure area during a fluid diversion, you can lose the chance to continue on that same departure even when the airline later decides to fly.

If crew really did send ticketed Tampa passengers to claim bags while others stayed for a continuation, that is a communications failure on top of weather. Keep receipts for a Delta exception request, a delayed-bag claim, or travel-insurance paperwork. Receipts document the expense; they are not a promise that Delta will reimburse a rental car, ride-hail, hotel, or other ground move under its normal weather policy.

## What to do on a diversion

1. Stay behind TSA until the airline confirms whether the flight will continue, cancel, or offload bags. A specific carousel number sounds like a cancellation. Ask a gate agent or flight attendant to confirm before you leave security.
2. If you are already outside security and TSA is closed, call Delta and open the Fly Delta app for the next available rebook to Tampa. There is no useful RSW-TPA scheduled shuttle on Delta; ground transport is often DIY when Fort Myers is the divert field, though Delta may still arrange a ground move at its discretion.
3. File a delayed-bag report if your checked luggage went to Tampa without you. Keep hotel, meal, and ground-transport receipts for documentation even when weather is the coded cause. Card [flight-delay insurance](https://flytrippers.com/most-important-things-related-to-canceled-and-delayed-flights/) sometimes helps when the delay meets that card’s terms.
4. If the domestic LAX-Tampa leg is part of the same international itinerary or carriage into the US, Montreal Convention delay-expense claims can be a separate conversation from domestic weather amenities. Weather and the carrier’s reasonable-measures defense can still limit recovery. File with documentation; it is not a same-night voucher or a likely payout.

## Bottom line

Delta 749 diverted LAX-Tampa into Fort Myers on Saturday in Tampa thunderstorms, then continued to Tampa later that night. Passengers who say they followed a baggage-claim instruction ended up outside security with TSA closed, while the aircraft and some bags left without them. On the next diversion, confirm the continuation plan before you leave the secure area behind TSA, then use Delta’s rebooking path and keep receipts for whatever ground move gets you home.
