Air Canada, WestJet, and Air Transat asked a House of Commons committee to let the federal government stop a strike before it starts. [Bill C-39](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/C-39), still in committee, would still wait until a walkout is underway, and tickets you already hold do not change.

WestJet's CEO used [this summer's cabin-crew strike](/news/westjet-cabin-crew-72-hour-strike-notice/) as the example: [cancellations started before the walkout and continued after the deal](/news/westjet-flights-still-disrupted-after-cabin-crew-strike/). Airlines say that is why waiting until a strike starts is too late for passengers.

## What the bill would do

The Building Canada Strong Act had first reading on **September 21st, 2026**, and second reading on **October 8th**. Parliament's [LEGISinfo page](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/C-39) still lists it at committee.

[Justice Canada's Charter statement](https://justice.canada.ca/eng/csj-sjc/pl/charter-charte/c39_1.html) and the [first-reading text](https://www.parl.ca/DocumentViewer/en/45-1/bill/C-39/first-reading) would replace section 107 of the [Canada Labour Code](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/L-2/). Once a lawful strike or lockout is underway, the minister could direct the Canada Industrial Relations Board to order operations to resume, extend the collective agreement, or impose a binding way to settle the dispute. Two conditions would apply: the minister must have considered a special mediator's report, and must be of the opinion that the stoppage adversely affects, or may adversely affect, the national interest.

The minister could weigh significant economic impact, serious social disruption, and the effect of that direction on freedom of association. The amendments would not generally prohibit strikes.

The bill would also add a 21-day special mediator. The report would be made public. The stated aim is at least **10 days** between that publication and a stoppage. Appointing the mediator would not, by itself, pause the right to strike.

## What the airlines asked for

The [National Airlines Council of Canada](https://www.ourcommons.ca/Content/Committee/451/CC39/Brief/BR14293386/br-external/NationalAirlinesCouncilofCanada-e.pdf), which represents Air Canada, Air Transat, Jazz Aviation, and WestJet, told the committee in an **October 20th** brief that airlines have to cancel flights, move aircraft and crews, and notify passengers before a legal stoppage. NACC wants the minister able to act where a strike or lockout "occurs or may occur," if a special mediator finds a stoppage imminent and the minister finds a national-interest risk.

On **November 3rd**, [Air Canada vice-president of labour relations Michael Abbott](https://www.ourcommons.ca/DocumentViewer/en/45-1/CC39/meeting-7/evidence), Air Transat senior director Irene Chrisanthopoulos, and WestJet CEO Alexis von Hoensbroech repeated that ask in testimony.

Von Hoensbroech said that for WestJet, even a one-minute strike means about **four days** of disruption: roughly two days to wind the network down and two days to rebuild it. He said cancellations began before the **August 2nd** cabin-crew strike and continued after the **August 3rd** deal.

Chrisanthopoulos said the Code's [72-hour strike notice](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/L-2/section-87.2.html) is too short to wind down an international network and bring travelers home on the airline's own aircraft. She asked for a longer notice period for airlines. She did not name a replacement length.

## Railways and unions

The [Railway Association of Canada](https://www.railcan.ca/news/rac-statement-on-the-building-canada-strong-act-bill-c-39/) made the same core ask on **September 24th**: the bill still lets government react only after a strike or lockout has started. [Teamsters Canada](https://teamsters.ca/blog/2026/11/10/cn-and-cpkc-lobbyists-continue-push-for-strike-bans-in-rail-sector/) said on **November 10th** that CN asked senators last week to amend C-39 so Ottawa could order binding arbitration before a strike begins. The union opposes that.

The [Canadian Labour Congress](https://canadianlabour.ca/governments-case-for-restricting-workers-rights-doesnt-pass-fact-check-clc/) says Ottawa has not shown that new strike-ending powers would make the economy more stable. Citing Employment and Social Development Canada figures, it says working days lost in federally regulated private-sector industries, including ports, railways, and airlines, fell about **35%** from **127,810** in 2023 to **83,150** in 2025. Those figures do not measure the value of interrupted trade. The CLC [wants the new section 107 powers removed, and any decision to end a legal strike put to Parliament](https://canadianlabour.ca/clc-brings-solution-to-parliamentary-committee-as-bill-c-39-threatens-labour-stability/).

## What this means for travelers

Nothing material should change for a trip you already booked: the bill is not law, and no airline posted a new strike notice because of these hearings.

If the bill passes as drafted, Ottawa could still step in only after a walkout starts. Airlines say that is too late for passengers, because they cancel flights during the wind-down. Under the airlines' requested amendment, the minister could act before a stoppage only if a special mediator found it imminent and the minister found a national-interest risk. Unions say that would weaken the leverage that produces a deal at the table.

US and Canadian travelers on Air Canada, WestJet, Air Transat, or Jazz-operated Air Canada Express flights could see those pre-strike cancellations if one of those airlines prepared for a stoppage. Porter is not in the airline council that made the ask.

## What to do

There is nothing to rebook for this bill. If your airline later posts a strike notice, use that airline's advisory and rebooking path first.

## Bottom line

Air Canada, WestJet, and Air Transat asked MPs to let Ottawa stop a strike before it starts. Bill C-39 as drafted still waits until a walkout is underway, and it is still in committee, so tickets you hold do not change.
