Canadians with a residential mortgage can now earn Aeroplan points on that payment through [Chexy](https://flytrippers.com/go/chexy/). The fintech’s mortgage product, with Aeroplan earning terms effective August 4th, 2026, pays your lender from a Canadian chequing account or an eligible Visa Debit or Debit Mastercard and credits **1 Aeroplan point per $1 CAD** remitted. Credit cards are not allowed on mortgages here. Chexy charges a **1.75%** service fee on the payment amount.

## How the mortgage earn works

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Earn rate | **1 Aeroplan point per $1 CAD** paid to your lender (fee excluded) |
| Fee | **1.75%** of the payment amount (example: $43.75 CAD on a $2,500 payment) |
| Funding | Canadian chequing account / pre-authorized debit, or eligible Visa Debit / Debit Mastercard |
| Credit cards | **Not eligible** for mortgage payments on Chexy |
| Status | **1 Status Qualifying Credit (SQC) per 5** Aeroplan points earned, up to **25,000 SQC** per calendar year in Everyday Partners (base points only) |
| Points posting | After the payment completes; often up to about **60 days** to show in Aeroplan |
| Who can enroll | Canada residents of age of majority with an Aeroplan account in good standing |

[Chexy’s how-it-works page](https://intercom.help/chexy/en/articles/16193885-mortgage-payments-with-chexy-how-it-works) and [mortgage Aeroplan terms](https://intercom.help/chexy/en/articles/16194204-chexy-mortgage-payments-aeroplan-points-earning-terms-and-conditions) are the primary rules. In practice, mortgage funding is set up from a Canadian chequing account. The legal terms also mention eligible Visa Debit / Debit Mastercard, but expect the chequing path when you enroll.

Chexy buys the Aeroplan points and posts them as a promotional benefit. Your mortgage rate, lender, and amortization stay the same; you point the lender at Chexy’s account details so the pull comes from there once the lender’s change is effective.

This is separate from Chexy’s older rent, tax, and bill flow, where many people use a credit card and also collect card rewards on top of Chexy’s Aeroplan bonuses. On mortgages, the Aeroplan points come from the payment itself, not from charging a credit card.

I’ve used Chexy to pay taxes with a credit card since they launched that option, which is still the play on those non-mortgage payments where card funding is allowed. Mortgages are the opposite setup: the points come from Chexy on a bank-funded payment, not from putting the mortgage on plastic.

Per Chexy’s product pages, mortgage earn also credits Everyday Partner SQC (1 SQC per 5 base Aeroplan points, capped at 25,000 SQC per calendar year). That matches [Air Canada’s current Aeroplan partner SQC rules](https://www.aircanada.com/ca/en/aco/home/aeroplan/status.html) for Everyday and travel partners (base points only unless otherwise specified).

## What this means for travelers

If you already chase Aeroplan for Air Canada and Star Alliance trips, a mortgage is usually one of the largest monthly dollars that never hit a rewards card. Chexy’s product puts that spend into the program, including SQC toward Elite Status.

Watch the fee. At 1.75%, a $2,500 CAD payment costs $43.75 CAD for 2,500 points (about 1.75¢ CAD per point before you count SQC). That only makes sense if you value the points (and status progress) at least that high on the trips you actually book. Run the math for your payment size and how you redeem Aeroplan before you redirect your mortgage payment to Chexy.

If you already use Chexy for rent or bills, mortgage setup lives in the same account, but the mortgage payment still has to come from a bank account, not a credit card.

For the partnership announcement and deeper fee/card strategy on the *non-mortgage* payments that still allow credit cards, see [Flytrippers’ Aeroplan-Chexy partnership coverage](https://flytrippers.com/aeroplan-chexy-partnership/) and the [Chexy guide](https://flytrippers.com/chexy-guide/).

## What to do if you want in

1. Confirm you have a free [Aeroplan](https://www.aircanada.com/aeroplan) membership and that the last name matches your Chexy profile exactly, including formatting and case.
2. Start at [Chexy’s mortgage flow](https://flytrippers.com/go/chexy/), connect the chequing funding method (or an eligible Visa Debit / Debit Mastercard if that option appears), and **link your Aeroplan number before the first payment completes**.
3. Give your lender the Chexy account details. Chexy’s [switch guide](https://intercom.help/chexy/en/articles/16194070-how-to-switch-your-mortgage-payments-to-chexy) says some lenders take up to 10 business days, so start at least two weeks before the next payment, confirm the lender’s effective date, and keep the old account open and funded until a payment has successfully cleared through Chexy. Do not assume the next scheduled pull will already use Chexy.
4. Keep enough balance for the payment **plus** the 1.75% fee once Chexy is the funded account.
5. Track points in Chexy’s Aeroplan view and in your Aeroplan account; expect a lag of up to about 60 days after completion.

Chexy also still advertises a separate limited-time Aeroplan bonus of up to **7,000** points for new qualifying Chexy users (first-payment and monthly payment bonuses), with an offer window ending September 30th, 2026. Treat that as its own offer with its own conditions; do not assume every mortgage payment automatically fills that bonus stack.

## Bottom line

Chexy now lets Aeroplan members in Canada earn **1 point per dollar** on residential mortgage payments funded from a bank account or eligible Visa Debit / Debit Mastercard, for a **1.75%** fee, with Everyday Partner SQC on the base points. The mortgage itself does not change. Whether it is worth it depends on your monthly payment size and how you use Aeroplan.
