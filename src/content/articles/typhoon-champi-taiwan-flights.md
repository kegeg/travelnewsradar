Taiwan's Civil Aeronautics Administration canceled all Friday, October 23rd, 2026 domestic flights as Typhoon Champi passed east of the island. [AirAsia](https://newsroom.airasia.com/news/airasia-flights-impacted-by-typhoon-champi) is offering affected guests a refund, a 730-day credit, or a free date change on Taipei (TPE) and Kaohsiung (KHH) tickets for October 23rd and 24th. Airlines also canceled hundreds of international and cross-strait flights, so check your flight before you leave; Saturday schedules are still being retimed.

This is the same check-your-booking habit as [Hurricane Lala in Hawaii](/news/hurricane-lala-hawaii-flights-waivers/) and [Hurricane Isaias in Florida](/news/pensacola-destin-airports-closed-hurricane-isaias/).

## What happened

[Taiwan's Civil Aeronautics Administration](https://www.caa.gov.tw/NewsPublish-Content.aspx?a=381&lang=1&nid=2591) said Thursday that all domestic flights on Friday would be canceled. By 6 p.m. Friday, [Taiwan's Central News Agency](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202610230220.aspx) reported that CAA's Champi response team counted 262 canceled domestic flights and 724 canceled international and cross-strait flights, plus 9 delays.

[China Airlines](https://www.china-airlines.com/tw/en/prepare-for-the-fly/information/announcements) warned that flights to or from Okinawa on October 22nd, and flights to or from Taiwan from the morning of October 23rd through the morning of October 24th, could leave early, delay, or cancel. [Singapore Airlines](https://www.singaporeair.com/en_UK/sg/corporate/newsroom/newsalert-listing/advisory-on-singapore-airlines-flights-affected-by-typhoon-champ/) canceled its four Singapore-Taipei flights on Friday (SQ876, SQ877, SQ878, and SQ879). [STARLUX](https://latestnews.starlux-airlines.com/en-Global/about-us/travel-advisories/advisories/latest-news/flights-notice-2026) said some flights may change and told passengers to use its online form because phone lines are busy.

Saturday morning, [China Airlines' schedule-change board](https://e-comm.china-airlines.com/typhoon_status/SchChg.html) still showed leftover cancellations and a long list of Friday flights pushed into Saturday, including Singapore and Bangkok arrivals into Taoyuan.

[Taiwan's Central Weather Administration](https://www.cwa.gov.tw/V8/E/index.html) still had sea and land typhoon warnings up at 10:15 a.m. Saturday, October 24th. Champi's center was near 25.5°N, 123.2°E, east of northeastern Taiwan, with maximum sustained winds of about 78 mph (126 km/h) and gusts to about 101 mph (162 km/h). It was moving north, then northeast, near 13 mph (21 km/h). The forecast takes the center farther north by early Sunday.

## What this means for travelers

If you were booked on a Taiwan domestic hop Friday, that flight did not operate. If you are booked to, from, or through Taipei, Kaohsiung, or another Taiwan airport this weekend, open the airline app before you go. Saturday flying is coming back in places, with leftover delays and cancellations.

If the airline canceled your flight, stay on its rebooking path first (app, manage booking, airport desk, or a partner flight it assigns). Wait for that offer before you buy a new ticket on another carrier. If your flight is still showing, use a posted waiver only if you want to move dates while the fee-free window is still open.

People already in Taiwan should watch wind, rain, and road conditions, not just the drive to the airport.

If you are still booking after this weekend, check the live airline schedule rather than an old itinerary.

## Airline change windows

Rules differ by ticket number, purchase date, original travel date, and whether the flight was already canceled. Open manage booking before you pay a change fee.

**AirAsia** (codes AK, FD, Z2, and D7) covers Taipei and Kaohsiung on October 23rd and 24th. Affected guests can take a full refund to the original form of payment, a credit valid 730 days in the AirAsia member account, or one free same-route date change, subject to seats. AK, FD, and Z2 can move within 30 days of the original departure. AirAsia X (D7) can move within 90 days. Check the AirAsia MOVE app, AskBo, or the email on the booking.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app or manage booking before you go to the airport.
2. Recheck the [Central Weather Administration warning](https://www.cwa.gov.tw/V8/E/index.html) if you are already in Taiwan. Road times can still run long while the warning is up.
3. Look for a travel waiver in manage booking before you pay a change fee. EVA's listed change deadline is October 24th. Cathay's listed deadline is before October 24th.
4. If the airline canceled your flight, take its reaccommodation (including partners) before you buy a new ticket on another carrier.
5. Keep receipts for meals or hotels in case your airline or insurance provider asks for them.

If you paid with a card that includes flight-delay insurance, that coverage can still matter for hotel and meal costs after a long enough delay. For the practical checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

Taiwan canceled all Friday domestic flights for Typhoon Champi, and the Civil Aeronautics Administration's Friday evening count was 262 domestic and 724 international and cross-strait cancellations. AirAsia is offering a refund, a 730-day credit, or a free date change on Taipei and Kaohsiung tickets for October 23rd and 24th. China Airlines, EVA Air, Cathay Pacific, and Delta have their own windows, with different cutoffs. Check your flight, use the airline's rebooking path first, and use a waiver in manage booking before you pay change fees. EVA's listed change deadline is October 24th.
