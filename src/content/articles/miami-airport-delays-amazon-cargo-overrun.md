Miami International Airport (MIA) is still delaying inbound flights on **Tuesday, September 8th, 2026**, after an Amazon cargo jet overran a runway Sunday afternoon. The [FAA](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) has a ground delay program through **9:59 p.m. Eastern**, with average inbound waits of **131 minutes** (about 2 hours 10 minutes), and [the airport](https://www.miami-airport.com/) says two of four runways remain closed.

As with [O'Hare storm ground stops](/news/chicago-ohare-storm-ground-stops-waivers/) and [Northeast storm and controller-staffing delays](/news/faa-northeast-ground-stops-atc-staffing/), start with your airline's live status before you leave for the airport.

## What happened

An Amazon Prime Air Boeing 767-300 operated by 21 Air overran runway 30 at MIA around 2 p.m. Eastern on Sunday, September 6th, arriving from San Juan, Puerto Rico. The jet struck vehicles on the ground, killing five people and injuring five others. The National Transportation Safety Board is investigating and said the people who died were in the vehicles that were hit.

The FAA stopped all arrivals and departures while crews responded. That full ground stop ended Sunday evening, when two runways reopened and passenger flying resumed. The airport is still short two runways, and aircraft and crews that never arrived on Sunday are still catching up.

A ground delay program is the FAA holding Miami-bound flights on the ground at their origin airports so they do not show up faster than MIA can land them. The [Tuesday operations plan](https://www.fly.faa.gov/adv/adv_spt) lists that program as active because of runway and taxiway constraints. The live NAS board currently codes the reason as a disabled aircraft on the runway. Those program times can move.

## What this means for travelers

If you are booked to, from, or through Miami today, the schedule can still slip even though the airport is open. Inbound flights are being held at their origin. Missed connections and late aircraft can linger after a Labor Day weekend disruption, especially on American, which uses MIA as a major Latin America and Caribbean hub.

If the airline canceled your flight, stay on its rebooking path first (app, manage booking, airport desk, or a partner flight it assigns). Wait for that offer before you buy a new ticket on another carrier. If your flight is still showing, use a posted waiver only if you want to move dates while the fee-free window is still open.

I have connected through MIA on American and Air Canada more than once. A short-runway day there usually shows up later as missed connections once aircraft and crews are in the wrong cities.

If you use American's option to move to Fort Lauderdale or another Florida airport, check that airport's status too. The FAA also lists Fort Lauderdale as a possible thunderstorm ground stop later today.

## Airline waivers still in play

[American's travel alerts page](https://www.aa.com/web/i18n/travel-info/travel-alerts.html) still lists a Miami waiver (information current as of September 7th). As published there this morning:

- American Airlines flights to, from, or through MIA
- Any fare class, including Basic Economy
- Ticket bought by September 6th, 2026
- Originally scheduled travel September 6th through 8th, 2026
- New travel September 6th through 12th, 2026, same cabin (or pay the fare difference)
- You can keep the same cities, or change origin or destination to Fort Lauderdale, Fort Myers, Key West, Orlando, Sarasota/Bradenton, Tampa, or West Palm Beach
- Changes must be booked by September 8th, 2026

That rebook deadline is today. Eligible trips may show a change option in manage booking. If you cannot complete the change online, call American Reservations.

Other airlines that fly Miami may have opened their own short windows. Open manage booking on your carrier before you pay a change fee.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app or manage booking.
2. Recheck the [FAA NAS status](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) and [MIA's homepage notice](https://www.miami-airport.com/) the same day. Ground delay end times can move.
3. If you qualify for American's waiver and your plans are flexible, change it today while that book-by date is still open, online or through Reservations.
4. If the airline canceled your flight, take its reaccommodation before you buy a new ticket on another carrier.
5. Keep receipts for meals or hotels if you get stuck overnight.

Under [DOT refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), a cancellation or significant delay or schedule change is a different conversation from a simple delay if you choose not to travel. If you paid with a card that includes flight delay insurance, that coverage can still matter for hotel and meal costs after a long enough delay. For a practical checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

Miami is open, but the FAA is still holding inbound flights at their origin airports on Tuesday after Sunday's Amazon cargo overrun, with average waits around two hours 10 minutes and two runways still closed. Check your flight before you go. If you are on American and eligible for the Miami waiver, the fee-free change has to be booked today, in manage booking or through Reservations.
