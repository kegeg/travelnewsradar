Nadi International Airport (NAN), Fiji’s main international gateway, is short on usable Jet A-1 after a recent delivery from one supplier that cannot be used. The airport is open. Carriers including Fiji Airways, Air New Zealand, Virgin Australia, and Qantas are working around limited uplift with tankering fuel (carrying extra fuel in from elsewhere so they do not need to buy it at Nadi), bigger aircraft, technical stops, and some cancellations. If you have a Fiji flight in the next couple of weeks, check status before you leave for the airport.

## What happened

Deputy Prime Minister and Civil Aviation Minister Viliame Gavoka said Nadi is dealing with a Jet A-1 supply-chain problem, a Notice to Airmen (NOTAM) is out, and usable fuel is being prioritized for contracted operators such as Fiji Airways plus medical evacuations. Operators outside that priority list are expected to uplift fuel at ports outside Fiji. Fuel suppliers have told officials stocks should normalize by the end of August. Gavoka also said there is no fuel contamination.

Fiji Airways called it a minor, temporary jet fuel supply constraint after a recent delivery from one of its suppliers that cannot presently be used. The airline said it has already secured alternative supplies, does not anticipate disruption to flights or guests, and will not use fuel until it clears its testing and clearance process. It said it is coordinating with government agencies, Fiji Airports Limited, and industry partners.

[Fiji Airports](https://fijiairports.com/) said aviation fuel at Nadi is supplied and managed by fuel service providers, and that matters of fuel quality, availability, and supply sit with those providers and the relevant authorities. The airport operator said Nadi remains open and operational, it is monitoring fuel availability and expected supply, and airlines manage their own fuel and operational adjustments.

Some early reporting described a contaminated batch, including an unconfirmed claim about an excessive electrical charge in stored fuel. The cause remains unconfirmed. The minister has denied contamination, and Fiji Airports has not confirmed the reported excessive-electrical-charge explanation.

Airline workarounds already show up for travelers:

- Air New Zealand said that over the last two days it tankered fuel, canceled one service, and switched two services to larger widebody aircraft, with affected passengers moved to other same-day flights. Its current travel alert says Nadi flights are expected to operate as scheduled while it monitors fuel availability.
- Virgin Australia said Nadi services have been refueling in Port Vila or Nouméa, with some flights also canceled.
- Qantas has been using Airbus A330 aircraft on Sydney-Nadi so the aircraft can avoid needing a full Nadi refuel for the return, according to the airline’s comments to reporters.

Pilots on some carriers have been told the constraint could run through the end of the month, which matches the official normalize-by-end-of-August window.

## What this means for travelers

Nadi is not closed. The risk is schedule change on international routes into and out of Fiji while usable Jet A-1 is tight. Fiji Airways says it expects to keep flying with alternative fuel lined up and priority access to fuel. Foreign carriers are more likely to tanker fuel from Australia or New Zealand, add a technical stop (for example Port Vila or Nouméa), swap to a larger aircraft, or cancel and reaccommodate same day.

If you are already in Fiji on a return ticket with Air New Zealand, Virgin Australia, Qantas, or another foreign carrier, watch for same-day cancel and rebook notices, plus longer airborne times if the plane stops for fuel elsewhere. If you are still inbound, the trip can still operate, but the aircraft you board and the routing may differ from what you booked.

Leisure travelers from Canada and the US usually reach Fiji on Fiji Airways or via Australia or New Zealand connections. Either path can move while the shortage lasts. There is no verified fee-free change waiver list tied to this fuel issue as of publish time, so do not assume you can move dates for free unless your airline posts one.

## What to do

1. Check your live booking in the airline app or manage booking before you leave for the airport.
2. Prefer [Fiji Airports](https://fijiairports.com/) and your airline’s own alerts for Nadi operational updates. Use [Air New Zealand’s travel alerts](https://www.airnewzealand.com/en-nz/travel-alerts), [Virgin Australia](https://www.virginaustralia.com/), [Qantas](https://www.qantas.com/), or Fiji Airways status tools for your ticketed carrier.
3. If the airline cancels your flight, take its reaccommodation first (including partners) before you buy a second ticket.
4. Keep receipts for meals or hotels if you get stuck overnight. Card flight-delay insurance can help when the delay meets that card’s terms. For the broader checklist, see [Flytrippers on canceled and delayed flights](https://flytrippers.com/most-important-things-related-to-canceled-and-delayed-flights/).

## Bottom line

Nadi remains open, but usable Jet A-1 is constrained after a delivery that cannot currently be used. Fiji Airways says it has alternative fuel and does not expect guest disruption. Other carriers are tankering, switching to larger aircraft, adding tech stops, or canceling and rebooking same-day passengers. Check your Fiji booking through the end of August until suppliers say stocks are back to normal.
