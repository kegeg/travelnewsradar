Dubai International Airport (DXB) and Abu Dhabi’s Zayed International Airport (AUH) are open on **Wednesday, September 2nd, 2026**, but arrivals and departures are running behind after overnight US-Iran military strikes. Emirates, Etihad, flydubai, and Air Arabia have delayed, canceled, or diverted individual flights, and several foreign carriers continue to keep UAE routes suspended or thinned. If you are flying today, check your booking before you leave for the airport.

Another Middle East aviation story this week, after [Air Canada’s Dubai and Tel Aviv suspension into 2027](/news/air-canada-dubai-tel-aviv-suspension/) and the earlier [Aqaba airport alert in Jordan](/news/aqaba-airport-alert-jordan-travel/).

## What happened

Reporting overnight and into Wednesday morning points to a fresh exchange of fire between the United States and Iran, including US strikes on Iranian targets and Iranian missile and drone attacks aimed at US interests in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Jordan. UAE airspace remained open, along with Bahrain, Kuwait, and Jordan, but airline schedules across Dubai and Abu Dhabi still slipped as operators adjusted for regional airspace risk and knock-on delays.

Airport boards and same-day airline updates showed a mixed picture: many Gulf routes still operating, plus a long list of late Emirates and Etihad long-haul and regional flights, selected Air Arabia cancellations (including some Kuwait and Bahrain services), and at least one Etihad diversion tied to an airport closure elsewhere on the network. Timings remain subject to change at short notice.

[EASA’s conflict-zone bulletin for Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman airspace](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/air-operations/czibs/czib-2026-07r2) (CZIB-2026-07R2) stays valid through **September 30th, 2026**. It applies to EASA operators and certain EASA-authorized third-country operators. Over Persian Gulf waters in the Bahrain, Kuwait, Doha, and Emirates FIRs, it advises operators not to fly except as needed for arrivals and departures. Over Gulf of Oman waters west of longitude 58°E in the Muscat FIR, it advises no operations at any altitude. Over land in those FIRs, operators should fly cautiously and watch for missile, drone, and debris risk. That bulletin helps explain why many EASA-regulated carriers still keep UAE routes suspended or thinned even when DXB and AUH are open. Other airlines follow their own risk assessments and post their own suspension windows.

## What this means for travelers

If you hold a ticket on Emirates, Etihad, flydubai, Air Arabia, or another airline still serving DXB or AUH today, expect delays, cancellations, and short-notice schedule changes on Wednesday. Your flight may still go, but later than printed, or with a short-notice cancellation. Connections through Dubai or Abu Dhabi have little slack when the hub is catching up.

If you are booked on a carrier that has not resumed UAE flying, that suspension still applies on its own dates. [Air Canada’s Middle East travel update](https://www.aircanada.com/ca/en/aco/home/book/travel-news-and-updates/2026/middle-east-travel.html) (dated August 4th, 2026) still keeps Dubai canceled until mid-January 2027. Other North American and European airlines continue to post their own UAE suspension windows into the fall. Use your airline’s live advisory, not a generic “Dubai is open” headline.

[Global Affairs Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/united-arab-emirates) still advises Canadians to **avoid non-essential travel** to the United Arab Emirates. That page was updated **September 1st, 2026**, and notes that military activity can cause flight cancellations and other travel disruptions. Advisories are one planning input, not a full on-the-ground forecast. I was in Dubai and Abu Dhabi with Caroline in February, when day-to-day city life felt far quieter than the regional headlines. The security picture has shifted since then.

People already in the UAE should follow local instructions and their airline’s messages. People still deciding whether to keep a leisure trip should weigh the live advisory, insurance wording, and whether their airline is actually operating the dates they hold.

## What to do now

1. Open your airline app or manage-booking page and confirm **your** flight’s status before you leave for DXB or AUH. Use [Emirates flight status](https://www.emirates.com/english/manage-booking/flight-status/), [Etihad flight status](https://www.etihad.com/en/manage/flight-status/), and the [Dubai Airports flight board](https://dubaiairports.ae/flight-status) or [Zayed International Airport](https://www.zayedinternationalairport.com/) as backups, not replacements for the carrier.
2. If the airline cancels or significantly delays your flight, take its rebooking path first (including partner flights) before you buy a second ticket on your own.
3. Build extra time for check-in, security, and road traffic if your airline or the airport asks you to, or when the board is already late. Online check-in helps when it is still open.
4. Recheck [Canada’s UAE advice](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/united-arab-emirates) and your own government’s page the same day you travel. Turn on airline alerts for the next few days; another escalation can move schedules again with little warning.

## Bottom line

Dubai and Abu Dhabi airports are operating on September 2nd, but Wednesday’s board is messy after renewed US-Iran strikes. Check your live booking, use the airline’s rebooking tools if the flight moves, and read the current UAE advisory before you assume a leisure trip is routine this week.
