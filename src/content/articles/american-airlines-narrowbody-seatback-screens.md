American Airlines said on **August 18th, 2026** that it will install seatback entertainment screens on new narrowbody deliveries and on aircraft it plans to retrofit, while growing premium seating on those planes. Nothing about today’s bookings changes. New Airbus and Boeing deliveries get the screens starting in **2028**, with the retrofit wave aimed at finishing by the **early 2030s**. Some older planes set for retirement are not expected to get screens.

American also recently added [seasonal JFK flights to Calgary and Québec City](/news/american-airlines-jfk-calgary-quebec-city/). That schedule news is separate from today’s cabin plan.

## What American announced

In its [August 18th newsroom release](https://news.aa.com/news/news-details/2026/American-Airlines-announces-fleetwide-upgrade-with-installation-of-seatback-screens-MKG-OB-08/default.aspx), American said it will:

- Put next-generation seatback screens at every seat on **new Airbus and Boeing narrowbody deliveries starting in 2028**, and on retrofitted aircraft after that
- Aim to finish that screen rollout by **early next decade**
- Grow **premium and Main Cabin Extra** seats from about **25%** of seats on narrowbody departures today to about **40%** in the coming years

American already has seatback screens on more than **140** long-haul aircraft. The new push is about the shorter-haul mainline fleet that many US travelers actually fly. An American spokesperson has said about **100** older A319s, the A320 fleet, and other narrowbodies headed for retirement will not get the screen retrofit.

## Seatback screens: timing and features

American says the new displays will include **4K** picture quality, Bluetooth headphone pairing, USB-C charging at every seat, personalized content recommendations, and interactive flight maps. It also says the screens will be among the largest on narrowbody aircraft in North America.

Those features matter once your aircraft has them. Until the 2028 delivery and retrofit wave starts reaching the plane you booked, bring a charged device and plan to stream or download the way most American domestic flyers do now. No-screen narrowbodies are still common on a lot of American’s short-haul flying today.

## More First Class and Main Cabin Extra

American is already retrofitting **Airbus A319 and A320** aircraft to add another row of First Class. Upcoming **Boeing 737 MAX 10** deliveries are planned with **24** First Class seats, and the **A321neo** fleet is set to be reconfigured with more First Class seats as well.

Separately, American says it will retrofit most of the narrowbody fleet to expand **Main Cabin Extra**, which brings extra legroom, earlier boarding, access to overhead bin space, and complimentary drinks on flights with full drink service.

Refreshed interiors (more storage, redesigned seats, enhanced lighting) are part of the same cabin push. Exact seat maps will still vary by aircraft subtype and retrofit wave, so check the seat map for your flight rather than assuming every American narrowbody looks the same.

## Starlink and other cabin investments

In the same release, American said it will start installing **Starlink** Wi-Fi on narrowbody aircraft beginning in **2027**. Lounge expansion, dining partnerships, and digital booking tools are also listed as part of a broader customer-experience spend. Those lines are useful context for American’s broader spend, separate from when a screen actually reaches your seat.

## What this means for travelers

For trips in **2026 and 2027**, this is a multi-year product roadmap. Your ticket rules, bag fees, and schedule do not change because of this announcement.

When you care about entertainment or a specific premium seat:

- Check the **aircraft type and seat map** at booking and again before departure
- Do not assume a 2028 delivery promise means your 2027 flight has screens
- If you want First Class or Main Cabin Extra, buy that product when it is offered on your itinerary, or request an eligible upgrade when American makes one available; more inventory later does not free-upgrade today’s Main Cabin ticket

## Bottom line

American plans seatback screens on new narrowbody deliveries from **2028** and on aircraft it retrofits through the **early 2030s**, while growing premium and Main Cabin Extra share toward about **40%** of narrowbody seats. For travel you are booking now, plan for the cabin you see on the seat map.
