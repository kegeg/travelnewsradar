A construction contractor damaged a fiber-optic line at Houston's George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) on Tuesday evening, October 13th, 2026, and [Houston Airports](https://www.fly2houston.com/iah/alerts/) said flights were seeing significant delays. The FAA held inbound flights and then canceled that ground stop, so check **your flight** if you were booked to, from, or through IAH tonight or Wednesday morning.

This is the same check-your-booking habit as September's [Newark-area fiber and equipment outage](/news/newark-jfk-laguardia-flights-resume-faa-outage/). A ground stop is the FAA holding IAH-bound flights at the airport they are leaving from, so they do not show up faster than Houston can land them.

## What happened

Houston Airports posted at 5:30 p.m. Tuesday that a construction contractor had damaged a fiber-optic line. It warned of significant delays, said cancellations were possible through the evening, and told international arrivals they could wait longer at US Customs. Crews were on site making repairs. The airport said it was coordinating with airlines and Customs and Border Protection.

The [FAA's IAH status page](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=IAH&p=1) coded the problem as Other during the event and said flights bound for Houston would not be allowed to leave their origin until at or after 7:00 p.m. Central. The [NAS board](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) listed an IAH ground stop from 6:25 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. Eastern (5:25 p.m. to 7:00 p.m. Houston time). The FAA also opened a [Texas coordination line](https://www.fly.faa.gov/adv/adv_otherdis?adv_date=10132026&advn=90&facId=ATCSCC) for equipment issues at Houston-area air traffic facilities, including Houston TRACON (I90) and IAH.

IAH is United's Houston hub, so United connecting itineraries may be especially exposed. Flight-tracking tallies late Tuesday showed more than 300 delays at IAH and only a handful of cancellations.

At 8:00 p.m., Houston Airports said Customs wait times for international arrivals were back to normal. Crews were still repairing the line, and delays and cancellations remained possible that evening.

A later [FAA operations plan](https://www.fly.faa.gov/adv/adv_otherdis?adv_date=10142026&advn=10&facId=ATCSCC) said the situation at IAH was improving, some systems were coming back, and the ground stop had been canceled. Houston-area facilities were still working out how many arrivals they could take. The plan listed extra recovery work through 11:00 p.m. Central for IAH flights that had been sent to other airports. The [Texas hotline](https://www.fly.faa.gov/adv/adv_otherdis?adv_date=10142026&advn=11&facId=ATCSCC) was then closed.

At 9:15 p.m., Houston Airports said operations were returning to normal as crews finalized repairs. It told passengers traveling Wednesday morning, October 14th, to check their flight status with their airline before heading to the airport, and said it anticipated a full recovery overnight.

As of about 9:30 p.m. Central, the FAA's IAH page showed no destination-specific delays and general gate-hold, taxi, and airborne delays of 15 minutes or less. That page is not flight-specific.

## What this means for travelers

If you were booked to, from, or through IAH on Tuesday evening, check **your flight** in the airline app before you go. The ground stop is canceled. Aircraft, crews, and connecting passengers can still be in the wrong cities after an evening hold at a hub.

If you connect at IAH on Wednesday morning, Houston Airports told passengers to check their flight status with the airline before heading to the airport. The FAA is not showing an active ground stop as of late Tuesday evening. A delayed Tuesday arrival can still mean you miss the onward flight.

If you are still booking a trip later this week, nothing material should change.

Canadian itineraries that connect at IAH take the same hit as a US domestic ticket. The same missed-connection risk applies even when your Canadian airport looks fine.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app or manage booking before you go to the airport. Do not rely on the FAA airport page alone.
2. Recheck the [FAA National Airspace System status](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) and the [IAH airport status page](https://www.fly.faa.gov/fly/flyfaa/flyfaaindex?ARPT=IAH&p=1) for a new ground stop that may not show in the app yet.
3. Look for a travel waiver in manage booking before you pay a change fee. [American's travel alerts](https://www.aa.com/i18n/travel-info/travel-alerts.jsp) had no Houston equipment notice as of late Tuesday. If you hold a United ticket through IAH, open [United's travel alerts](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/travel/trip-planning/travel-alerts.html) and manage booking rather than assuming a fee-free window.
4. If the airline canceled your flight, stay on its rebooking path first. Keep receipts for meals or hotels if you get stuck overnight.

Air traffic control equipment problems in the US generally do not trigger a standalone airline cash payment for a simple delay. Under [DOT refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), a cancellation or significant delay is a different conversation if you choose not to travel. If you paid with a card that includes flight-delay insurance, that coverage can still matter for hotel and meal costs after a long enough delay. For a practical checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

Houston Airports said Customs waits were back to normal by 8:00 p.m. and that it expected a full recovery overnight. The late-evening FAA board showed only light delays. Check your flight if you were booked through IAH tonight or Wednesday morning.
