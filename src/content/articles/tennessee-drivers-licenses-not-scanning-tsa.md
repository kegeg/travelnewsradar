Tennessee’s Department of Safety and Homeland Security says a **small number** of state driver’s licenses are **not being read by TSA** at airport checkpoints. TSA’s ID rules are unchanged, but some Tennessee cards are having trouble being read at the checkpoint. Most travelers with a working license should see a normal checkpoint. If your card fails at the reader, TSA says officers work as quickly as they can to verify you for screening, though that manual check can add time. If you have already hit this scan problem, get a free state replacement or bring another [acceptable ID](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/identification) such as a U.S. passport before your next trip.

The department’s **August 11th, 2026** notice said it contacted the vendor that produces the cards, is reviewing affected licenses, and is working to fix the issue. Exact technical cause is still under review. Traveler anecdotes about light photos or other design quirks remain unconfirmed until Tennessee or TSA publishes a root cause.

## What Tennessee is offering

Anyone who hits this scan problem can visit a [Driver Services Center](https://www.tn.gov/safety/driver-services/locations.html) and get a **replacement license at no cost**. The state framed the free replacement around people who have already experienced the scan failure. If your Tennessee license has already failed to scan at TSA, replacing the card before the next trip is the cleanest fix the state is offering right now.

## What happens at the checkpoint

TSA’s traveler-facing rule has not changed: adults still need an [acceptable ID](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/identification) at the checkpoint. REAL ID-compliant state licenses remain on that list, along with passports, passport cards, Global Entry / NEXUS / SENTRI / FAST cards, and other accepted documents.

When a Tennessee license has trouble at the travel document checker podium, TSA has said officers work as quickly as they can to verify the passenger for screening. That is manual identity work, which can add time in the lane. [TSA ConfirmID](https://www.tsa.gov/tsaconfirm-id) is the separate **$45** option for travelers who arrive **without** an acceptable ID. A scan failure on a presented Tennessee license does not automatically put you on that fee path.

## What this means for travelers

If you hold a **Tennessee driver’s license** and it has scanned fine lately, nothing material should change for the trip. Keep the same airport buffer you already use.

If your Tennessee license has already failed to scan, the state wants you at a Driver Services Center for a free replacement. Carry a U.S. passport or another accepted ID as backup until the new card is in hand.

If you are flying with someone on a Tennessee license and you are not, their delay can still slow the whole party through the document check. Build a little buffer if that is your group, especially on a tight connection.

Canadian and other foreign travelers using a passport at U.S. checkpoints are outside this license-scan issue.

## What to do

1. If a Tennessee license has already failed to scan at TSA, get the **free replacement** at a [Driver Services Center](https://www.tn.gov/safety/driver-services/locations.html) before your next flight when you can.
2. Bring a **U.S. passport** or another [accepted ID](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/identification) as backup, especially if you have already had this scan failure.
3. Allow a bit of extra time at security if you know your card has been hard to read. ConfirmID’s **$45** fee applies when you arrive without an acceptable ID.

## Bottom line

Tennessee says a small number of licenses are not being read by TSA and is offering free replacements to people who hit this scan problem while it works with its card vendor. Most travelers should still clear security on a normal ID. If your card has already failed to scan, replace it or bring a passport so a slow manual check is not your only option on departure day.
