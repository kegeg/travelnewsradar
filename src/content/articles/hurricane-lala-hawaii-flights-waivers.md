Hurricane Lala shut Hilo International Airport (ITO) and Ellison Onizuka Kona International Airport (KOA) on Saturday, August 15th, and [Hawaii’s Department of Transportation](https://hidot.hawaii.gov/weather-traffic/) still listed both Hawaii Island airports as closed on Sunday morning, August 16th. Hawaiian, Alaska, Southwest, and Delta have opened temporary fee-free change tools for many Hawaii trips tied to the storm weekend. If your itinerary touches the islands, check flight status and manage booking before you leave for the airport.

This is the same check-your-booking habit as other summer disruption weeks, including [Chicago O'Hare storm ground stops and waivers](/news/chicago-ohare-storm-ground-stops-waivers/) and [Northeast storm and controller-staffing delays](/news/faa-northeast-ground-stops-atc-staffing/).

## What happened

The Hawaii Department of Transportation closed ITO Saturday morning and KOA Saturday afternoon as Lala moved past Hawaii Island. HDOT said it expected staff back Sunday to resume operations, while telling passengers to confirm status with their airline. As of Sunday morning, its live weather and traffic board still showed both airports closed. Honolulu (HNL), Kahului (OGG), and Lihue (LIH) stayed open, with carriers cutting or trimming flights ahead of winds and rain.

[Hawaiian and Alaska reported](https://news.alaskaair.com/on-the-record/updates-on-tropical-storm-lala/) canceling 59 neighbor-island flights and 18 transpacific flights for Saturday, then raising the proactive Sunday cut to 17 neighbor-island and seven transpacific flights. Schedules on Maui and Kauai ran reduced; most Honolulu flying kept going with spot cancellations. Southwest also warned of delays and cancellations Friday through Sunday at ITO, HNL, OGG, KOA, and LIH.

The storm was a hurricane through Saturday night and the National Hurricane Center downgraded it to a tropical storm early Sunday, though some pages and headlines still say Hurricane Lala. Use the live waiver and flight-status pages either way.

## What this means for travelers

If you are booked to, from, or through Hawaii for August 14th through 16th, the schedule can still move until the airline confirms the flight is operating. Hawaii Island is the hardest hit because Hilo and Kona were closed. Neighbor-island and some flights to and from the mainland are also at risk while aircraft and crews recover.

If the airline canceled your flight, stay on its rebooking path first (app, manage booking, airport desk, or partner flight it assigns). Wait for that offer before you buy a new ticket on another carrier. If your flight is still showing, use a posted waiver only if you want to move dates while the fee-free window is still open.

People already on Hawaii Island should watch for continuing flash-flood risk, road closures, and widespread power outages, not just the drive to the airport. Hawaii County has an [active search-and-rescue operation](https://www.hawaiicounty.gov/Home/Components/News/News/4631/720) after flooding near Naʻalehu. Check road and power status separately. Some roads can stay blocked even after the airport reopens.

## Airline waivers still in play

Rules differ by ticket issue date, original travel date, allowed new dates, cabin, and whether Alaska or Hawaiian issued the ticket. Open manage booking before you pay a change fee.

[Alaska and Hawaiian’s flexible travel board](https://www.hawaiianairlines.com/content/advisories/flexible-travel-policies) lists a Tropical Storm Lala waiver for travel to or from HNL, ITO, KOA, LIH, and OGG. For Alaska-issued tickets (13-digit numbers starting with 027) purchased on or before August 13th, 2026, original travel August 14th through 16th can move into new travel dates August 13th through 19th with no change fee and no fare difference in the same cabin, if you change or cancel before the original departure. Guests on Hawaiian flights are directed to Hawaiian chat, text, or 1-800-367-5320.

[Southwest’s travel advisory](https://www.southwest.com/travel-advisory/) covers Hurricane Lala for the same five Hawaii airports on August 14th through 16th. Eligible customers may rebook or travel standby within 14 days of the original travel date between the original city pairs without paying a change in airfare, or keep the existing reservation. If Southwest cancels or significantly delays the flight and you choose not to travel, refund rules on that page still apply.

[Delta’s Tropical Storm Lala advisory](https://www.delta.com/us/en/advisories/weather-alerts/tropical-storm-lala) covers travel to, from, or through HNL, KOA, LIH, and OGG on August 15th and 16th for tickets issued on or before August 14th. Reissued travel that begins on or before August 19th in the same cabin can waive the fare difference when booking class is maintained; later dates may still waive the change fee with a possible fare difference. Delta’s posted list does not include Hilo (ITO).

Other carriers that fly Hawaii may open their own short windows. Recheck your airline’s alerts the same day you change anything.

## What to do

1. Check whether **your flight** is delayed or canceled in the airline app or manage booking.
2. Confirm Hilo and Kona status on [HDOT’s weather and traffic page](https://hidot.hawaii.gov/weather-traffic/) if those airports are on your itinerary.
3. If you qualify for a waiver and your plans are flexible, move while seats remain rather than waiting for the last rebook inventory.
4. If the airline canceled your flight, take its reaccommodation (including partners) before you buy a new ticket on another carrier.
5. Keep receipts for meals or hotels in case your airline or insurance provider asks for them.

US weather cancellations and significant delays are a different conversation from a simple delay under [DOT refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds). If you paid with a card that includes flight delay insurance, that coverage can still matter for hotel and meal costs after a long enough delay. For the practical checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

Hurricane Lala closed Hilo and Kona on Saturday, and HDOT still showed both closed Sunday morning while other Hawaii airports kept operating with cuts. Hawaiian, Alaska, Southwest, and Delta have posted fee-free change tools for eligible storm-weekend Hawaii travel, with different date and ticket rules. Check your flight, use the airline’s rebooking path first, and use a waiver in manage booking before you pay change fees.
