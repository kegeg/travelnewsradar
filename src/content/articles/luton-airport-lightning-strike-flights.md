A lightning strike hit air traffic control equipment at London Luton Airport (LTN) on Thursday evening, August 27th, 2026, and the airport suspended operations for about 95 minutes. About 30 flights were canceled or diverted. As of Friday morning, August 28th, Luton says it is [open and fully operational](https://www.london-luton.co.uk/passenger-info), but airlines can still be cleaning up delayed and canceled trips.

As with [O'Hare storm ground stops](/news/chicago-ohare-storm-ground-stops-waivers/) and [Narita access cuts after record rain](/news/narita-airport-record-rain-stranded-travelers/), start with your airline's live status.

## What happened

Luton said operations were suspended between **17:40 and 19:15 BST** (12:40 to 2:15 p.m. Eastern) after lightning affected ATC equipment. Flights then resumed, with the airport warning that some delays and cancellations could continue while airlines recover schedules.

The same storms brought flash flooding on local roads and on the airport approach. NATS, which provides ATC at Luton, said severe thunderstorms disrupted flights at both Luton and Stansted that evening.

Same-day reporting also tracked inbound diversions, including Wizz Air flights that landed at Gatwick and Stansted instead of Luton. Exact passenger counts and overnight hotel arrangements vary by airline and ticket.

## What this means for travelers

If you were booked through Luton on Thursday evening, the airport shutdown is over, but you may still be dealing with missed connections, late arrivals, diverted landings, and rebooking queues. Friday flights are running again, but aircraft and crews can still be out of place after a same-evening shutdown.

If you fly Luton today or over the weekend, the airport is open. Check **your flight** first. Residual cancellations are possible even when the passenger-info page says fully operational.

Luton is a common London gateway for easyJet, Wizz Air, Ryanair, and other short-haul leisure routes. I have used it that way myself on easyJet and Wizz, so the pattern is familiar: when LTN takes a weather or ATC hit, the knock-on is often more about the evening bank and next-day recovery than a multi-day airport closure.

Stansted travelers should also double-check Friday status after the same severe thunderstorms, even if their airport did not post the same lightning-equipment suspension.

## What to do

1. Check **your flight** in the airline app or site before you leave for the airport. Rebooking starts with the carrier that holds the ticket.
2. Recheck Luton’s [passenger information](https://www.london-luton.co.uk/passenger-info) page and [departures](https://www.london-luton.co.uk/departures) board the same day.
3. If you were canceled or diverted Thursday, stay on the airline’s rebooking path. Keep receipts for meals or hotels if you got stuck overnight.
4. For flights covered by UK261 or EU261, severe weather can qualify as an extraordinary circumstance that may remove eligibility for fixed compensation. Whether an individual ATC-related disruption qualifies is case-specific, while care and rebooking duties can still apply. Use the airline claim path and the [CAA cancellations page](https://www.caa.co.uk/passengers/resolving-travel-problems/delays-and-cancellations/cancellations/) for the rules that match your ticket.
5. If your card includes flight-delay insurance, that coverage can still matter for hotel and meal costs after a long enough delay. Thresholds vary. For a practical disruption checklist, see [what to do when a flight is canceled or delayed](https://flytrippers.com/6-things-to-do-when-your-flight-is-canceled-or-delayed/).

## Bottom line

Lightning knocked out ATC gear at London Luton on Thursday evening and paused flights for about 95 minutes, with about 30 cancellations and diversions. Friday the airport is open again. Confirm your airline status before you travel, and expect some leftover schedule mess while airlines catch up.
